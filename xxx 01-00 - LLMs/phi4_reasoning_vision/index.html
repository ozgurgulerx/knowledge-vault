    Phi-4-Reasoning-Vision-15B¶

    See also: LLM Landscape & Trends | Multimodal & Real-Time | Post-Training & Inference-Time Techniques | AI Engineering Practices

    Paper: arxiv.org/html/2603.03975v1 | Release: March 2026 | License: Open-weight (permissive)

    Opening Briefing¶

    The conventional assumption in multimodal AI is that competitive vision-language performance requires trillion-token training budgets and 30B+ parameter counts. Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B dismantles this assumption by achieving frontier-competitive results on mathematical reasoning, chart understanding, and GUI grounding with just 200 billion multimodal tokens — roughly one-fifth of what Qwen3-VL or Gemma-3 consumed — and at 15 billion parameters. The key insight is not architectural novelty but a disciplined application of three compounding principles: start from a reasoning-capable language backbone rather than bolting reasoning onto a vision model after the fact, invest disproportionately in data curation over data volume, and let a single model switch between fast direct answers and chain-of-thought reasoning using explicit mode tokens rather than training separate models for each behavior.

    The data curation pipeline is where the real leverage lives. Microsoft manually classified every training dataset into quality tiers — excellent, fixable, or excludable — then regenerated answers for good-questions-with-wrong-answers using GPT-4o/o4-mini, programmatically repaired formatting errors, and augmented sparse domains with synthetic records. This produced a 62.8M-sample instruction-tuning set where each sample had been touched by human judgment at the classification level. The result: on ScreenSpot-V2 (GUI grounding), Phi-4-reasoning-vision outscores Gemma-3-12B by 85 points (88.2 vs 3.5) and trails Qwen3-VL-32B by only 5.7 points despite being half its size. On ChartQA it matches Qwen3-VL-32B within 0.7 points. The dynamic-resolution SigLIP-2 vision encoder capped at 3,600 tokens — roughly 720p native resolution — proved critical: ablations showed it delivered the best uniform performance across mathematical reasoning and high-resolution perception tasks, outperforming multi-crop and S2 alternatives.

    The broader implication is that the Pareto frontier for vision-language models has shifted. A 15B model running on a single H100 with sub-4K output tokens now occupies a practical deployment sweet spot where latency, cost, and accuracy intersect favorably against models requiring 10x more compute. This makes Phi-4-reasoning-vision a reference architecture for teams building multimodal agents — computer-use agents, document processors, scientific assistants — who need reasoning capability without frontier-model pricing. The document below maps the architecture, training recipe, data strategy, and benchmark profile that produced this result.

    Architecture¶

    Mid-Fusion Design¶

    Phi-4-reasoning-vision uses a mid-fusion architecture — vision and language are processed by separate encoders, then fused via a learned projection before the language model's transformer layers.

    Pipeline: Image → SigLIP-2 (dynamic resolution, NaFlex variant) → MLP projector → Phi-4-Reasoning LLM (15B)

    • Vision encoder: SigLIP-2 with dynamic resolution, capped at 3,600 visual tokens (~720p native)
    • Cross-modality projector: MLP layer that maps SigLIP-2 visual embeddings into Phi-4-Reasoning's text embedding space
    • Language backbone: Phi-4-Reasoning (pretrained on 400B unique tokens for Phi-4 base, then 16B tokens of reasoning training)
    • All spatial coordinates normalized to [0.0, 1.0] relative to image dimensions

    Vision Encoder Selection (Ablation)¶

    Dynamic resolution at 3,600 max tokens was selected based on systematic ablation:

    Method Max Tokens MathVista ScreenSpot ScreenSpot-Pro V*Bench
    Dynamic-S2 3,096 42.9 78.4 9.4 52.9
    Multi-crop 3,096 43.4 67.8 5.4 51.8
    Multi-crop + S2 2,048 43.4 79.1 10.6 57.1
    Dynamic resolution 2,048 45.2 81.5 9.2 51.3
    Dynamic resolution 3,600 44.9 79.7 17.5 56.0

    Key: Dynamic resolution with more visual tokens (3,600) nearly doubled ScreenSpot-Pro accuracy vs the 2,048-token variant (17.5 vs 9.2). High-resolution perception is a prerequisite for high-quality reasoning — the model cannot reason about what it cannot see.

    Training Pipeline¶

    Three-Stage Recipe¶

    Stage 1 — MLP Pretraining (alignment) - What trains: MLP projector only (vision encoder + LLM frozen) - Data: 2.0M samples, 1.4B tokens - Purpose: Align SigLIP-2 visual space with Phi-4-Reasoning text embeddings - Config: LR 1×10⁻³ cosine, batch 1024, max seq 2,048, AdamW (β₂=0.999), no weight decay

    Stage 2 — Instruction Tuning (full unfreeze) - What trains: All components (MLP + vision encoder + LLM) - Data: 62.8M samples, 188.5B tokens - Purpose: Multimodal instruction-following with mixed reasoning/non-reasoning - Config: LR 2×10⁻⁵ cosine (0.1 min ratio), batch 1,920, max seq 8,192, AdamW (β₂=0.95), weight decay 10⁻⁴ - Reasoning mix: ~20% reasoning data (with <think>...</think> CoT traces), ~80% non-reasoning (with <nothink> token for direct responses)

    Stage 3 — Long Context, Multi-Image & Safety - What trains: All components - Data: 3.2M samples, 12B tokens - Purpose: Extend to 16K context, multi-image reasoning, sequential screenshots, safety alignment - Config: LR 7×10⁻⁷ cosine, batch 1,920, max seq 16,384

    Total multimodal training: ~200B tokens (vs 1T+ for Qwen3-VL, Kimi-VL, Gemma-3)

    All stages use bf16 mixed precision, DeepSpeed ZeRO-1, gradient clipping at 1.0, warmup 3% (Stage 1) or 500 steps (Stages 2-3).

    Reasoning Strategy¶

    Why Start from a Reasoning Backbone¶

    Microsoft evaluated four training approaches:

    1. Non-reasoning LLM → reasoning multimodal training
    2. Non-reasoning LLM → non-reasoning multimodal → reasoning multimodal
    3. Reasoning LLM → all-reasoning multimodal (100% CoT traces)
    4. Reasoning LLM → mixed reasoning/non-reasoning multimodal ← selected

    Starting from a reasoning-capable LLM backbone means the multimodal training grounds existing reasoning ability in visual contexts rather than trying to teach reasoning and vision simultaneously. The mixed approach (20% reasoning, 80% direct) lets the model deliver fast answers for simple tasks and chain-of-thought for complex ones.

    Mode Tokens¶

    • <think>...</think> — wraps chain-of-thought reasoning steps (used for ~20% of training data)
    • <nothink> — signals direct response without reasoning trace (used for ~80%)
    • At inference, the model learns to switch between modes based on query complexity
    • Limitation: the learned switching is not always optimal — forcing thinking mode improves some benchmarks (MathVerse: 53.1 vs 44.9 default; MMMU: 55.0 vs 54.3)

    Data Curation¶

    Quality-First Philosophy¶

    Key: "The most substantial improvements come from systematic filtering, error correction, and synthetic augmentation — reinforcing that data quality remains the primary lever for model performance."

    Every dataset was manually classified into tiers: - Excellent quality — kept unchanged - Good questions, wrong answers — answers regenerated using GPT-4o / o4-mini - Low-quality questions — excluded or fully regenerated - Low-quality images — excluded - Good quality, formatting errors — fixed programmatically

    Data Augmentation Techniques¶

    • Detailed image descriptions generated for math/science datasets (multiple records per image)
    • Double-duty data — QA pairs reformatted for instruction-following in specific output formats
    • Multi-image training — scrambled caption matching and caption-to-image matching from grouped images
    • "What's changed?" data — sequential screenshots for navigation/computer-use tasks
    • Prompt variation — varied human prompt phrasing to teach robustness

    Math vs Computer-Use Data Balance (5B Ablation)¶

    Experiments with a 5B variant tested data ratios: - General baseline: 1M pairs - Math data: 150K–450K (with 3× duplication tested) - Computer-use: 150K–850K + Phi-Ground dataset (400K)

    Finding: A single model can achieve uniformly superior performance across math reasoning and computer-use domains simultaneously — no catastrophic forgetting trade-off. The Phi-Ground grounding dataset yielded the most impressive improvements on ScreenSpot-V2.

    Efficiency Claim¶

    "Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B pushes the Pareto frontier of the trade-off between accuracy and compute costs. We achieve competitive performance with much slower models that require ten times or more compute time and tokens, and better accuracy than similarly fast models."

    • Tested on NVIDIA H100, single-thread, batch size 1
    • Max output tokens: 4,096
    • Positioned as the practical sweet spot for interactive multimodal agents

    Safety Evaluation¶

    Modality Defect Rate
    Text-to-text 1.4%
    Image-to-text 4.5%

    Evaluation included automated red teaming on Azure across categories: disallowed content (sexual, violent, hateful, self-harm), copyright/IP, jailbreak susceptibility, groundedness, and fabrication.

    Key Insights¶

    1. Data quality > data quantity — 200B curated tokens competitive with 1T+ uncurated tokens. Manual dataset classification + synthetic answer regeneration + programmatic formatting repair collectively matter more than scale.

    2. Reasoning backbone first, vision second — Starting from a reasoning-capable LLM and grounding it in visual contexts outperforms trying to teach reasoning and vision simultaneously.

    3. Mixed mode > forced mode — A single model with explicit <think>/<nothink> mode tokens handles both simple and complex queries better than separate specialized models, though the learned switching is imperfect.

    4. High-resolution perception enables reasoning — The model cannot reason about what it cannot accurately perceive. Dynamic resolution at 3,600 tokens (~720p) was the minimum for reliable high-resolution grounding (ScreenSpot-Pro jumped from 9.2 to 17.5).

    5. Multi-domain without forgetting — Math reasoning and computer-use grounding can coexist in a single 15B model without catastrophic interference, if data proportions are balanced.

    6. Grounding data is high-leverage — The Phi-Ground dataset produced the largest single improvement on GUI grounding benchmarks, suggesting that spatial grounding is a learnable skill that transfers across screen types.

    Limitations¶

    • Larger proprietary models outperform on broad, unconstrained vision-language benchmarks
    • Learned reasoning/non-reasoning mode switching is not always optimal
    • Limited on extremely detailed or nuanced image understanding
    • Optimal 20/80 reasoning/non-reasoning ratio may not suit all domains
    • Ideal data balance remains an open research problem
    • Text-conditioned image tokenization (for efficiency) not yet explored

    Open Research Directions¶

    • Text-conditioned image tokenization for token efficiency
    • Data proportion effects at saturation scale
    • Extreme data imbalance effects (1% or less ratios)
    • Q-Former and attention-based cross-modal approaches for token reduction
    • Optimal reasoning mode switching (learned vs prompted)
