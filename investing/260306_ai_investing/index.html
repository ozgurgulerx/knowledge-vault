    AI Investing Ecosystem — March 2026¶

    Executive Summary¶

    An AI capex supercycle that is too real to ignore (money already committed) and still fragile (ROI, power, and supply-chain physics are the binding constraints). Most investable opportunities for a moderate-risk investor skew toward "picks-and-shovels with pricing power": high-speed networking, advanced packaging + HBM, and power/cooling infrastructure.

    Key: When any link in the value chain tightens, the whole chain re-prices in favour of the scarce link. In 2026, the scarcity nodes are grid power + interconnect lead times + advanced packaging/HBM.

    Assumptions¶

    • Geographic focus: global (US + Asia semiconductor supply chain + EU regulation)
    • Risk tolerance: moderate (public equities + thematic baskets; selective private/VC as optional satellites)

    Prioritised Opportunity Areas (High to Medium Conviction)¶

    1. High-speed networking & interconnect — 800G -> 1.6T migration; durable even if training slows
    2. Advanced packaging + HBM supply chain — capacity/yield are strategic; pricing power persists
    3. Power + cooling + electrical distribution — DCPI surpassing $80B by 2030; liquid cooling ~$7B by 2029
    4. Systems + design software consolidation — hardware/software co-design enabling faster iteration

    Value-Chain Mental Model¶

    The AI value chain in 2026 is a coupled system, not a linear "chips -> models -> apps":

    1. Compute (accelerators + memory + packaging) — sets raw capability
    2. Interconnect (scale-up + scale-out + DCI) — sets utilisation and time-to-train/serve
    3. Power + cooling — sets deployable capacity and expansion pace
    4. Software/hardware co-design — sets realised perf-per-watt and perf-per-dollar

    The Biggest Non-Obvious Macro Constraint: Electricity¶

    The constraint is not global kWh — it is local MW + interconnection timelines.

    • IEA: data centres ~415 TWh (2024, ~1.5% of global electricity) -> ~945 TWh by 2030; ~15% pa growth
    • BCG: global DC power demand expected to reach ~130 GW in 2028 (16% CAGR from 2023-2028)
    • Dell'Oro: power availability becoming a binding constraint; on-site generation shifting from contingency to necessity for very large AI campuses

    Key: When supply is constrained by permitting + grid physics, the clearing price shifts to whatever (a) shortens time-to-power or (b) increases compute-per-watt. Electrical gear, cooling, and networking became high-multiple assets because they compress deployment time, not just add capacity.

    Compute Silicon & Packaging¶

    Structural Truths (2026)¶

    • Dell'Oro: global DC accelerator market reaching $382B by 2029; custom accelerators expected to outpace GPUs in volume over time
    • Constraint is increasingly packaging + memory, not only wafer supply:
    • Reuters: packaging capacity constraints are a bottleneck for shipping leading accelerators at scale
    • PwC on HBM: by 2028, HBM becomes a $38B segment with ~58% revenue CAGR (2023-2028), ~64% bit growth, ~half of server DRAM market
    • SK hynix (memory maker, South Korea): preparing HBM4 production systems; cross-compatibility challenges increase vendor lock-in

    Key Players & Economic Rent¶

    Player Category Key Financials Notes
    Nvidia (GPU designer, US) Merchant accelerators FY2026 revenue $215.9B; GAAP GM 71.1% (Q4: 75.0%) Clearest "system profit pool"
    AMD (x86 CPU designer, US) Challengers 2025 revenue $34.6B; GM 50%; DC net revenue $16.6B Diversified compute
    TSMC (foundry, Taiwan) Foundry oligopoly FY2025 GM 59.9%; HPC majority platform share in 2025 Fabless software-like margins on top of foundry oligopoly
    ASML (lithography, Netherlands) Critical equipment — Expanding beyond EUV into advanced packaging/stacking

    Revenue Models & Margin Logic¶

    • Accelerator designers: monetise via (1) chips, (2) systems/racks, (3) software ecosystems / developer lock-in ("CUDA-like" effect), (4) networking attach. Gross margins can look like software when supply is constrained.
    • Foundries & critical equipment: monetise capex cycles; margin stability depends on node leadership + utilisation and export controls
    • HBM + packaging: monetise scarcity + qualification lock-in; long qualification cycles and steep yield learning curves sustain pricing power when demand is inelastic

    Bottleneck Thesis: Invest "One Layer Down"¶

    Key: The best risk-adjusted exposure is often not the GPU itself, but components with (a) long qualification, (b) limited vendors, and (c) demand pulled through by every AI build-out.

    Bottleneck Why It Stays Tight Investable Entry Points Horizon
    HBM Qualification + stacked yields + limited top-tier capacity; HBM4 increases foundry involvement SK hynix; Micron (US); Samsung Electronics Medium
    Advanced packaging (2.5D/3D, CoWoS-like) Capacity + substrate + tooling + yield learning TSMC; ASE Technology (OSAT, Taiwan) Medium
    Test & burn-in consumables Higher power devices increase test complexity; consumables are "razor blades" Socket/probe-card ecosystem; selective specialists Medium
    Power delivery on-package Retimers, PMICs, high-current connectors Interconnect + power semiconductor basket Medium

    M&A Signals (Compute)¶

    • Synopsys (EDA, US) completed acquisition of Ansys (simulation, US) — system-level co-design consolidation
    • AMD completed acquisition of ZT Systems (AI systems integrator, US), keeping design while divesting manufacturing to Sanmina (electronics mfg, US) — "systems design" is strategic IP; high-volume manufacturing is a partner capability

    Regulatory/Geopolitical Risks¶

    • Export controls on advanced computing continue to shape addressable markets (Nvidia explicitly not assuming certain China DC compute revenue in guidance)
    • Geographic concentration risk around Taiwan remains a strategic factor for node-leading silicon exposure

    Data-Centre Interconnect & Networking¶

    Why Networking Is the "Silent Multiplier"¶

    AI clusters are large enough that utilisation becomes a network problem. If a $30M GPU cluster idles due to network bottlenecks, the network budget can double and still be ROI-positive.

    Market Size & Speed Transitions¶

    • Front-end Ethernet DC switches: >$100B sales over next 5 years (Dell'Oro); nearly 100M shipments of 800G and 1.6T switch ports by 2028 enabled by 51.2/102.4 Tbps chips
    • AI back-end networks: switch spending >$100B over 2025-2029; ports migrating to 800G (2025) -> 1.6T (2027) -> 3.2T (2030) (Dell'Oro)
    • Roadmap increasingly about reducing joules/bit — driving linear-drive optics and co-packaged optics

    Key: Networking is no longer only "scale-out." Scale-up fabrics (PCIe/CXL/UALink, NVLink-like) are becoming an investable silicon category as multi-rack AI systems scale.

    Key Players¶

    • Switching systems & software: Arista Networks (Ethernet switching, US), Cisco, Nvidia networking stacks. Revenue: hardware + software + support; margins improve with software attach and NOS differentiation.
    • Merchant silicon & optical DSP/engines: Broadcom (semis & software, US), Marvell-class suppliers. Revenue: silicon ASP + volume; cyclical risk but strong AI demand tailwind.
    • Optical & DCI: lasers, coherent optics, fibre pull-through (especially inter-DC fabrics).

    Component-Level Opportunities ("Boring That Compounds")¶

    Connectors + cables: - TE Connectivity (connectors & sensors, Ireland): FY2025 GM 35.2%; digital data networks = 13% of net sales with very high AI/cloud-driven growth - Amphenol (electronic connectors, US): FY2025 sales $23.1B; "exceptional" IT datacom growth; adjusted operating margin 26.2% - Thesis: higher speeds -> more lanes -> more interconnect density -> qualification + reliability premiums

    Fibre & optical connectivity: - Meta signed a deal worth up to $6B with Corning (glass & fibre, US) for fibre-optic cables for AI DCs through 2030, including manufacturing expansion - Prysmian (cable maker, Italy): management commentary indicates US optical cable capacity strain (qualitative scarcity signal)

    M&A Signals (Interconnect)¶

    • Nokia (telecom equipment, Finland) completed acquisition of Infinera (optical networking, US) — positioning for DC optical network "revolution"
    • Marvell Technology (DC semis, US) acquired Celestial AI (optical I/O "Photonic Fabric") and XConn Technologies (PCIe/CXL switching) — scale-up roadmap
    • Semtech (analog & mixed-signal, US) acquired HieFo (InP optoelectronics) for ~$34M — adding InP lasers/gain chips; verticalising optical module content for AI interconnect

    Standards & Geopolitical Risks¶

    • Export controls can shift network silicon volumes by region; standards battles (Ethernet vs InfiniBand vs proprietary scale-up) create winner-take-most dynamics
    • Optical Internetworking Forum increasingly central for interoperability (400ZR/800ZR), affecting adoption curves and vendor mix

    Power, Cooling & Physical Infrastructure¶

    The Investable Idea¶

    "AI factories" are grid projects disguised as IT projects — switchgear -> transformers -> redundancy -> distribution -> heat rejection, with IT hardware as the payload.

    Market Size & Growth¶

    Segment Forecast Source
    DCPI Mid-teens CAGR (2025-2030), surpassing $80B by 2030 Dell'Oro
    Liquid cooling ~$7B by 2029; roughly doubling in 2025 to near $3B Dell'Oro
    Leading-edge GPU TDP Projected to exceed 4,000W by 2029 Dell'Oro
    Cooling share of facility power ~7% (efficient hyperscale) to >30% (less efficient enterprise) IEA

    The wide efficiency dispersion means spend on thermal optimisation remains rational.

    Where Margins Sit¶

    Most of this stack historically earned "industrial margins." The AI shift is changing that because (a) lead times and (b) systems engineering complexity are increasing.

    • Vertiv (DC infrastructure, US): adjusted operating margin 23.2% in Q4 2025 — tighter market, higher-value mix
    • Eaton (electrical equipment, US): record segment margins; explicitly ties order acceleration/backlog to DC momentum

    Component Map: "One Turn of the Wrench Earlier"¶

    Slice What's Scarce Public Equity Examples Why It Can Outperform
    Electrical distribution Switchgear, breakers, busway, PDUs Eaton (ETN), Schneider Electric, ABB Pull-through from every new MW deployed; high switching costs once standardised in a campus
    Liquid cooling CDUs + integration capability Vertiv (VRT), nVent (NVT), Eaton (via Boyd Thermal) Moves from "option" to "requirement" as GPU TDP rises
    Fibre + copper inputs Cable capacity, conductors Corning (GLW), Prysmian (PRY.MI) Interconnect density + power-train build-out increases material throughput

    Additional detail: - Rack power distribution: busway, cabinet/rack PDUs, breakers — benefit from higher rack power; overhead busway becoming default in slab-based AI factories (Dell'Oro) - UPS + backup generation: redundancy scales with criticality; grid interconnection delays increase value of on-site bridging - Grid equipment (transformers/switchgear): chronic long lead times; manufacturing capacity and skilled labour constrained — hard to "fix" quickly

    M&A in the Physical Stack¶

    • Eaton agreed to acquire Boyd Thermal (thermal management) for $9.5B — adding liquid cooling tech for DC build-out; expected close Q2 2026
    • Vertiv agreed to acquire PurgeRite Intermediate (HVAC services) for ~$1B — strengthening liquid cooling services

    Key: These aren't random acquisitions — the market is paying up for end-to-end delivery capability (hardware + service + integration) because AI campuses cannot tolerate commissioning risk.

    The Energy Commodities Angle (Often Missed)¶

    • Global grid investment reaching $390B (2024), expected >$400B (2025)
    • AI data centres contributing to structural copper demand; estimated ~650k tonnes of copper by 2030 (Reuters)

    Key: A diversified "AI infrastructure" exposure can rationally include grid commodities and cable makers, not just semis.

    Software-Hardware Co-Design, Hyperscalers & Edge¶

    Three Buyer Types Driving the Ecosystem¶

    1. Hyperscalers — capex giants, custom silicon, global footprint (Alphabet, Amazon, Meta)
    2. Neo-cloud / AI-specialist infra — often accelerator- and power-constrained. Example: CoreWeave (AI cloud, US) forecasting very large 2026 capex tied to accelerated infra build-outs and energy procurement
    3. Sovereign AI and regional build-outs — increasingly relevant for policy and supply-chain localisation

    Key: Dell'Oro identifies "neo cloud" as the fastest-growing customer segment in AI back-end switching. Neo-clouds buy complete stacks, shifting profit pools toward integrators and networking vendors.

    Co-Design = Durable Software Economics¶

    Best co-design plays sell time-to-solution and performance-per-watt, not "developer tools." - Synopsys + Ansys: unifying silicon design, IP, and simulation/analysis for faster AI-powered product iteration - Google TPU cadence and integration into AI Hypercomputer: custom silicon as strategic product feature for performance and energy efficiency

    Edge AI & Distributed Inference¶

    Less hyped than DC, but investable — spend base is broader and less concentrated. - IDC: worldwide edge spending reaching $380B by 2028 (services portion notably large) — a second, more diffuse capex cycle benefiting rugged compute, connectivity, and industrial integrators

    Regulatory & Policy Risk¶

    • EU AI Act timeline and obligations create compliance/deployment frictions — can advantage incumbents with strong governance tooling
    • US export controls continue to impact supply, product segmentation, and inventory decisions for major AI hardware vendors

    Investment Instruments & ETFs¶

    Public equities (core): semis (accelerators, memory, networking), critical infrastructure (power/cooling), connectors/fibre.

    Thematic ETFs (satellites):

    SMH VanEck Liquid large-cap semiconductors
    BOTZ Global X Robots & AI theme

    Frontier Technologies¶

    Near-Term "Frontier" (Already Becoming Capex)¶

    Optical I/O, co-packaged optics, and photonics — pulled forward by power-per-bit constraints and rack-scale bandwidth needs.

    • Nvidia investing $2B each in Lumentum (photonics, US) and Coherent (photonics, US) for AI processor photonics capability
    • Semtech-HieFo acquisition: InP lasers/gain chips for optical module verticalisation
    • Marvell-Celestial AI acquisition: optical I/O for package/system/rack connectivity ("Photonic Fabric")
    • Dell'Oro expects LPO traction; co-packaged optics adoption accelerating

    Long-Horizon Option Value (Size as Venture-Like)¶

    • Neuromorphic: Intel's "Hala Point" system — event-driven compute as sustainability path for certain workloads
    • Quantum: IBM roadmap toward fault-tolerant quantum computing + quantum+HPC tooling milestones
    • Cryogenic computing: peer-reviewed literature argues potential for energy-efficient big-data processing (von Neumann, neuromorphic, quantum), but commercial pathways remain early

    Portfolio Playbook (Moderate Risk)¶

    Structured around the reality that infrastructure cycles overshoot then normalise:

    Sleeve Allocation What Rationale
    Core 50-65% Constraint winners: electrical distribution + cooling + networking + memory/packaging Captures volume regardless of which model/app wins
    Compute beta 15-25% Accelerator winners and/or broad semi ETFs Participates if spend cycle surprises upward
    Optionality 5-15% Photonics and scale-up fabrics (PCIe/CXL/UALink) Adoption likely but timing uncertain
    Venture-like frontier 0-5% Neuromorphic/quantum/cryogenic via specialised vehicles Size as "call options"

    Due Diligence Checklist¶

    1. Customer concentration & wallet-share: quantify hyperscaler exposure and renewal cadence; verify "one-off build" vs "refresh + expansion"
    2. Lead-time power: check backlog quality (cancellable vs firm), ASP trends, component/labour constraints
    3. Supply-chain choke points: identify single-vendor dependencies (HBM, substrates, optics lasers, transformers)
    4. Unit economics under lower utilisation: stress-test if hyperscaler capex growth slows (not collapses) — does operating leverage reverse sharply?
    5. Regulatory/geopolitical sensitivity: export-control exposure, cross-border manufacturing risk, regional subsidy dependence

    Watchlist Triggers (Leading Indicators)¶

    • Networking speed transition: vendor commentary on 1.6T ramp pull-forward (bullish optics + switch silicon) or slip (risk)
    • Packaging/HBM easing: credible signs bottlenecks materially loosen (bearish constraint rents; bullish compute volume)
    • Grid interconnection timelines: on-site generation becoming standard (bullish gensets/turbines + switchgear) vs accelerated permitting (reduces scarcity premium)
    • Hyperscaler capex revisions: upward extends the trade; downward compresses multiples fastest in capex-elastic names
    • Large strategic supply agreements (like Meta-Corning): clearest proofs of multi-year demand lock-in
