    GCP Partner Playbook¶

    Consolidated reference: partner landscape, program mechanics, success patterns, and GTM strategies for Google Cloud partnerships.

    1. Partner Tiers & Landscape¶

    Tier 1: Global System Integrators¶

    • Accenture -- 750k+ employees, 120+ countries. 2025 Global Partner of the Year (multiple categories). Joint Generative AI CoE with Google (500+ industry-specific GenAI use cases, 60+ accelerators). 8,700+ Google Cloud certified professionals.
    • Deloitte -- Serves ~90% of Fortune 500. Multiple 2024 Partner of the Year honors (NA Sales, EMEA Services, Security, Global Social Impact). Massive sales and engineering workforce.
    • Capgemini -- European-rooted global SI. 2024 Global Industry Solution Partner of the Year for Generative AI. Strong in financial services and insurance. "Agentic AI" program with Google.
    • Cognizant -- 350k+ employees. Global Breakthrough Partner (2024, 2025). Built "Google Cloud AI University" training 25,000 professionals. "Five Ps" framework (Plan, Pilot, Prove, Production, Perfect).
    • TCS -- India's largest IT services. Global Expansion Partner of the Year (2024). 24+ specializations, 95+ expertise badges (most of any partner). "Google Cloud Garage" innovation program. Five 2025 Partner of the Year awards.

    Tier 2: Large Global Partners¶

    • Wipro -- Global Breakthrough Partner of the Year (2025). FullStride Cloud Services for migration.
    • Infosys -- Application Development Partner of the Year (2024). "Cobalt" platform for app modernization.
    • Kyndryl -- 2024 Infrastructure Specialization Partner of the Year. Modernizing legacy systems (mainframes, VMware) onto GCP.

    Tier 3: Specialist / Pure-Play Partners¶

    • SADA (now Insight Enterprises) -- Google Cloud-only. 7+ Partner of the Year awards. Global Sales Partner of the Year (2024). $2.5B GCP consumption commitment. 10-11 specializations. SaaS Alliance Program (35 ISV partners, 350% growth in a year).
    • Quantiphi -- "AI-first" digital engineering. NA Services Partner of the Year (2024). 11 Google Cloud Partner awards since 2017. Healthcare AI, contact center AI, cybersecurity.

    European Standouts (EMEA)¶

    • Devoteam (France) -- EMEA Sales Partner of the Year four years running. "G Cloud" unit scaled across Europe. Five 2025 Partner of the Year awards.
    • Datatonic (UK) -- Founded 2015 as boutique data analytics firm. Now 10x Google Cloud Partner of the Year winner. Acquired Montreal Analytics (Canada) and Syntio (Eastern Europe). Won 2025 Services Partner of the Year (Data & Analytics, EMEA).
    • Artefact (France) -- 2025 AI Partner of the Year. Data/AI specialization.
    • Aliz (Hungary/Austria) -- 2023 Services Partner of the Year (Alps region). Partnered with PriceHubble and LKW WALTER.
    • Appsbroker (UK), Nordcloud (Nordics) -- Other notable EMEA partners.

    Rising Mid-Tier and Boutique Partners¶

    • 66degrees (US) -- Merger of Cloudbakers + Qwinix (2022). ~500+ staff. NA Expansion Partner of the Year (2019, 2024). PE-backed (Sunstone Partners).
    • CloudWerx (US) -- Founded mid-2020, "all-in on Google." Premier Partner in under a year. 10x YoY revenue growth by 2022. 100% customer retention. Co-founders include 12-year Google veteran.
    • DoiT International (Israel) -- Free cloud optimization services funded by resale margins. 150+ cloud architects. 2023 Sales Partner of the Year (sub-regional).

    Other Notable Partners¶

    • WWT ($20B SI), SHI ($12B), CDW ($20B+) -- Government/enterprise IT modernization
    • Slalom -- 12,000+ employees, human-centric delivery, 7 Google Cloud awards
    • Go Reply (UK/Ireland), Nordcloud (now IBM) -- EMEA cloud-native
    • Iret (Japan AI Partner 2025), SoftBank -- Japan leaders
    • Palo Alto Networks -- Security innovation on GCP
    • Box -- Business applications Partner of the Year

    Partner Advantage Program Tiers¶

    All top performers maintain Premier Partner status (highest performance benchmarks). Google is shifting to outcome-based tiers (Select / Premier / Diamond) emphasizing capacity (skills) and capability (validated customer success).

    2. Program Mechanics & Obligations¶

    Partner Types at a Glance¶

    Area Reseller (Sell) Services Partner MSP ISV (Build)
    Sales Resells GCP, merchant of record, co-sells with Google field teams Consulting/pre-sales, scopes projects (not reselling) Managed services contracts, works with Google on migration deals Markets software on/integrating with GCP, co-sells via Marketplace
    Implementation Minimal direct implementation Architects and deploys GCP solutions, handles migration/configuration End-to-end cloud lifecycle, migrates workloads, continuous optimization Product development, builds/maintains product on GCP
    Billing Manages cloud billing, tiered pricing/discounts Does not resell (Google bills customer for usage, partner bills for consulting) Often acts as reseller for managed clients Manages own GCP costs, relies on Marketplace billing
    Support Frontline support, triages issues, Google as backline Support during projects, handoff at completion 24x7 managed support with strict SLAs, proactive monitoring Technical support for their software

    Certifications and Expertise¶

    • Base Partner: minimum 2 employees with professional Google Cloud certifications
    • Higher tiers and specializations: more credentials required
    • Specializations: require multiple advanced certs + successful project case studies (often vetted by third-party assessors)
    • Must keep certifications current as team grows; Google conducts annual evaluations

    Partner Agreements and Compliance¶

    • All partners sign Google Cloud Partner Program Agreement (plus addendums)
    • Resellers have liability to pay Google for customer usage
    • Must adhere to Partner Code of Conduct and regional legal requirements
    • Google can downgrade partners who don't meet updated requirements
    • Must track performance (certifications, revenue, customer success) to maintain "good standing"

    Tools and Portals¶

    Portal Purpose
    Partner Advantage Portal Primary hub: training enrollment, tier tracking, deal registration, specialization applications, marketing resources, Earnings Hub (rebates, discounts, incentives)
    Partner Sales Console Reseller interface: provision subscriptions, monitor customer spend, create private offers
    Cloud Marketplace / Vendor Portal ISVs: publish listings, track transactions, manage private offers
    Google Cloud Console Technical: deploy/manage cloud resources, create customer projects, spending alerts
    Partner Care / Support Open Google technical support cases on behalf of customers; Partner Support Desk tickets
    Partner Marketing Studio Co-branded campaign materials
    Google Cloud Skills Boost for Partners Exclusive training platform

    Deal Management and Attribution¶

    • Deal registration is crucial for financial incentives (discounts, rebates)
    • Maintaining "attribution hygiene" (keeping opportunity data synchronized) emphasized for co-selling
    • Service partners use deal referral workflows for fees or MDF
    • Marketing Development Funds (MDF) available but require submitted plans/applications
    • Must uphold Google's branding guidelines

    ISV/Build-Specific Requirements¶

    • Product integration reviews and security scans for Marketplace listing
    • Must prove solution runs primarily on GCP (drives consumption)
    • Must maintain listing; failure can lead to re-review or delisting
    • Can pursue "Google Cloud Ready" or "Solution Validation" designations

    3. Success Patterns & Case Studies¶

    Repeatable Success Patterns¶

    1. Deep Partnership and Early Commitment -- Treat the Google relationship as strategic, not transactional. SADA, Devoteam, CloudWerx all went "all-in" early. Achieving Premier status quickly and earning multiple Specializations correlates with accelerated growth.

    2. Investment in Skills and IP -- Heavy up-front investment in certifications and reusable assets. CloudWerx's certification blitz in first year. Datatonic's Academy. SADA's 500+ certified experts. Build frameworks and accelerators packaging knowledge into tools.

    3. Customer-Centric Outcomes (Referenceability) -- Google partner awards mostly celebrate customer impact delivered. CloudWerx's 100% retention rate. Aliz's customer quotes about going "above and beyond." Referenceable clients are a priceless asset.

    4. Co-Sell Alignment and Market Focus -- Align GTM with Google's priorities. Target underserved segments or geographies. Devoteam: go-to in EMEA mid-market. CloudWerx: targeted greenfield startups in NA. Leverage Google Marketplace to accelerate sales cycles.

    5. Continuous Innovation and Adaptation -- Quick to pivot to new trends (Kubernetes, Anthos, hybrid cloud, generative AI). Build R&D group internally to experiment with new Google releases.

    Growth Trajectories¶

    • Early Entry -- SADA "grew up in the evolution of Google Cloud itself." Devoteam began over a decade ago. Early entrants gained credibility before hyper-growth phase.
    • Scaling via M&A and Funding -- 66degrees (merger + PE). Datatonic (PE + acquisitions). CloudWerx ($7M Series A).
    • Inflection Through Specialization -- Achieving specializations often marks a tipping point. Appsbroker gained Infrastructure specialization, then tapped for major bank migration.
    • Enterprise Path (Deloitte, TCS): cross-sell into existing enterprise client base. Startup Path (CloudWerx, DoiT): target digital-native SMBs, "land and expand."

    4. GTM Strategies & Differentiators¶

    Sales and Co-Selling Models¶

    • "All-In" Commitment: SADA (exclusive Google Cloud, $2.5B consumption pledge), CloudWerx (all-in on Google)
    • Dedicated GCP Business Units: Accenture's AGBG with thousands of practitioners. TCS: distinct cloud units per hyperscaler.
    • Proactive Sales: Mid-sized partners identify greenfield customers. CloudWerx: "we're not just receiving leads from Google."
    • Joint Business Plans: Formal plans with Google for co-selling, account mapping, pipeline sharing
    • Co-Marketing: Joint press releases, case studies, presence at Google Cloud Next

    Revenue Models and Margin Levers¶

    Revenue Stream Characteristics
    Professional Services One-time project revenue (T&M or fixed-price). Mid-sized specialists often achieve higher margins through reusable IP and lower-cost delivery centers.
    Managed Services Recurring revenue (24x7 monitoring, optimization, enhancements). Smooths project volatility.
    Resale and Consumption Resale margins typically 5-15% but pure recurring revenue. SADA, DoiT built businesses on this.
    Vendor Incentives MDF, rebate sharing with Google on large deals.

    Margin levers: Utilization of staff, blended rates, location mix, IP reuse, automation, internal training (Datatonic Academy to grow junior engineers at lower rates).

    Vertical Industry Focus¶

    • Financial Services: Deloitte (Generative AI), TCS (Industry Solutions)
    • Healthcare: PwC, Quantiphi (cancer detection AI)
    • Public Sector: Deloitte (digital government), WWT, Carahsoft
    • Retail: Cognizant, Datatonic (ASOS)
    • Gaming: Datatonic (Hiber)
    • Sustainability: Capgemini (2025), Publicis Sapient (EV charging with Renault)
    • Speaking the customer's industry language and offering vertical accelerators differentiates against generalists

    Emerging Trends: Agent Productization & Autonomous Workflows¶

    Two shifts are reshaping partner GTM as of early 2026:

    Agent workflows becoming SKU-like products. Multiple partners are packaging internal agent flows into repeatable offerings with fixed scope and standardised pricing — moving away from "custom AI project" toward productised agent systems with faster sales cycles. The underlying pattern is "agent = product unit": consulting becomes the distribution and customisation layer around a core agent module. Examples include vertical agent products such as "Customer 360 reasoning agent" or "supply chain anomaly explainer agent" sold as scoped deliverables rather than open-ended engagements.

    Gemini adoption shifting from copilots to autonomous execution. New partner demos increasingly remove human-in-the-loop steps entirely, focusing on end-to-end agent-driven workflows: ticket resolution without escalation, procurement loops fully automated, internal ops handled by chained agents. The copilot phase — where the model assists a human operator — is giving way to execution agents as the baseline expectation in partner-led implementations.

    Emerging Technology Leadership (AI/ML, Gen AI)¶

    • Quantiphi: "AI-first" since inception
    • CloudWerx: Launch partner in Google's Gen AI programs (Vertex AI, AI App Builder)
    • Datatonic: "AI Workbench" and pre-built ML pipelines
    • Being recognized thought leaders in AI/ML (blog posts, community presence, case studies) differentiates

    Proprietary Tools and Accelerators¶

    • Accenture: 60+ industry cloud accelerators, GenAI Garage workshops, MxDR for security
    • Deloitte: EDGE platform for government
    • TCS: "Google Cloud Digital Garage"
    • SADA: "Power" suite (PowerBI, PowerML), SaaS Alliance Program
    • Quantiphi: AI accelerators for cybersecurity (SecOps)
    • LTIMindtree: Infinity platform (100+ tools)
    • KPMG: "AI in Control" framework for responsible AI governance

    Typical Staffing and Roles¶

    Technical: Cloud Solutions Architects, Cloud Support Engineers (24x7 for MSPs), FinOps/Billing Specialists Sales/BD: Account Executives (co-sell with Google AMs), Partner Alliance/Channel Managers (own Google relationship) Delivery: Project/Cloud Delivery Managers, Marketing Managers (co-branded campaigns) Specialized: Cloud Security Architects, Data/ML Engineers, SREs, Practice Leads Executive: Cloud Practice Director/CTO, Finance Director, Compliance Officer

    Common Pain Points¶

    • Certification burden -- Keeping certs current with rapid Google product expansion; engineers pulled from billable work
    • Deal attribution -- Tracking influence across own CRM and Partner Advantage portal; channel conflict risk
    • Cash flow risk -- Resellers pay Google net-30 regardless of customer payment; currency fluctuations; thin margins
    • 24/7 support scaling -- Requires multi-geography hiring; enterprise SLAs as low as 15-min response
    • Evolving program requirements -- Tiers, incentives, specializations change yearly; smaller partners struggle when bar is raised
    • Marketplace discoverability -- ISVs find it hard to stand out; engineering effort for integration and re-certification
    • Lead generation catch-22 -- New partners need deals to prove themselves but need track record to get deals

    Gaps and Opportunities for New Entrants¶

    1. Niche Specialization in Under-Served Domains -- Graph analytics, knowledge graphs, fraud networks, recommendation engines. Build reference architectures and accelerators.
    2. Enhanced FinOps for GCP in EMEA -- Full-service FinOps: advisory, automated waste cleanup, budget governance.
    3. Geographic Focus -- Eastern Europe, Middle East, Africa have fewer local Premier partners. Public sector EMEA and SMBs still underserved.
    4. Productized Solutions on Marketplace -- Package services as products (e.g., "Cloud Foundation Build in 4 weeks").
    5. Multi-Cloud/Hybrid Centered on Google -- Google-first but not Google-only. Anthos for hybrid, Apigee for multi-cloud API management.

    Sources: Google Cloud Partner of the Year Awards (2024-2025), Google Partner Advantage program documentation, partner case studies and press releases.
