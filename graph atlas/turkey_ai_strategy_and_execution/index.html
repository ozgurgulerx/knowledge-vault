    Turkey AI Strategy and Execution Plan¶

    Graph Atlas positioning as a "production AI systems + inference engineering" consultancy for regulated Turkish enterprises.

    Part 1: Strategic Analysis¶

    Executive Summary¶

    Key: Turkiye is entering a data-centre and sovereign-cloud build cycle (2026-2029) that is "stack-shaping": multiple large projects landing in Ankara and other hubs. The global AI business model is increasingly inference-cost constrained. Durable value is shifting to systems engineering for inference economics (latency, throughput, cost-per-token, availability, governance).

    Recommendation (12-24 months): Position as a "production AI systems + inference engineering" consultancy for regulated and latency-sensitive Turkish enterprises, with:

    1. Google Cloud practice timed to the Turkiye region announcement and new partner program (outcome-based tiers)
    2. Inference engineering moat: optimisation and LLMOps across NVIDIA-centric fleets (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, Triton)
    3. Repeatable "vertical accelerators": KVKK-compliant RAG + evaluation harness + audit logging + latency/cost SLOs for banking, telecom, public sector

    One-sentence positioning: "Graph Atlas makes AI economically and compliantly runnable in Turkiye -- across sovereign cloud, telco clouds, and hybrid GPU fleets."

    Turkey Market Context¶

    Policy Landscape¶

    • National AI Strategy (2021-2025): Targets 50,000 AI jobs and raising AI contribution to 5% of GDP
    • Twelfth Development Plan (2024-2028): Frames reduced foreign dependency; explicitly seeks to make Turkiye a regional data-centre base; includes Public Cloud Strategy and Action Plan
    • Ecosystem growth is policy-driven: Türkiye's AI momentum is structurally supported by visa reforms, global event hosting (TEKNOFEST, GITEX Türkiye), and infrastructure investment — with early signs of AI startup momentum emerging from this policy base. This fits a broader global pattern: governments in Türkiye, India, and the Middle East are building sovereign AI stacks to reduce dependence on U.S. platforms, creating parallel funding ecosystems with distinct regulatory and deployment constraints (Daily Sabah, Hürriyet Daily News, Invest in Türkiye, Bloomberg, 2026)
    • Cross-border startup pattern maturing: The ecosystem continues producing cross-border AI startups — HQ abroad (EU/US), engineering in Türkiye — with capital efficiency arbitrage as the key structural advantage. Early traction is clustering in three verticals: AI for gaming production pipelines (strong local talent base), industrial AI (manufacturing, supply chain), and SME-focused automation agents (regional market fit). A public-private push toward AI infrastructure sovereignty remains a central theme. Türkiye is currently behaving as an AI execution layer for Europe — a talent and cost-efficient hub for building and operating AI systems — not yet a capital concentration hub, but potentially a source of agent-based BPO 2.0 companies before producing frontier labs (TechCrunch, EU-Startups, Crunchbase, regional ecosystem reports, Mar 2026)

    Turkiye is transitional: "no in-country hyperscale cloud region" to "first hyperscale region arriving." No major hyperscale Turkiye region as of early 2026. Local "hyperscaler" function currently fulfilled by telcos + enterprise self-builds + wholesale entrants, until Google Cloud region reaches full capacity.

    Regulation, Data Localisation, and Incentives¶

    KVKK (Data Protection)¶

    • Amended March 2024: allows cross-border transfer with lawful basis + adequacy/safeguards
    • Enterprises need repeatable "KVKK-compliant GenAI reference architectures" and vendor/contract playbooks -- direct service opportunity

    Banking Regulation (BDDK)¶

    • Explicit about outsourcing governance and permitted cloud use
    • Finance = high-leverage entry vertical: budgets exist, compliance creates defensible complexity, outputs are measurable

    Data Centre Incentives¶

    • Investment incentives redesigned under 2025 decree: customs/VAT exemptions, tax reductions, land allocation
    • HIT-30 programme: minimum TRY 2bn investment; hyperscale criteria: (a) >=30 MW IT, (b) >=50% AI-compatible, (c) PUE <= 1.4, (d) integration with large cloud/AI centres
    • Incentives accelerate colocation build-out, increasing demand for tenant-side AI platform engineering

    Global AI Trends Shaping Demand¶

    Inference as Centre of Gravity¶

    • GPT-3.5-equivalent inference cost dropped hundreds-fold (2022-2024), yet remains a material marginal cost at scale
    • Durability concentrates in the layer that controls latency/cost/reliability

    Open-Weight Models¶

    • Llama and similar models make "self-host or managed-host" a mainstream enterprise option
    • Shift spend from "API tokens" to "GPU fleet + serving stack + operations": continuous batching, KV-cache, quantisation, speculative decoding

    NVIDIA Stack¶

    • TensorRT-LLM: high-performance LLM inference on NVIDIA GPUs
    • vLLM + PagedAttention: throughput gains via KV-cache efficiency
    • Systematic inference optimisation remains valuable as models change

    Managed Model Platforms¶

    • Vertex AI Model Garden: 200+ models; Claude and Llama available as managed APIs
    • Enterprises get continuum between "fully managed" and "self-deployed"
    • Turkish enterprises selling into EU often align to EU-style governance

    Programme Architecture¶

    Three separate vehicles to finance and partner without mixing risk profiles:

    Vehicle Description Revenue Model Capital Intensity
    A: PlatformCo (Managed Inference + MLOps) Software-led; compliant, low-latency inference via signed capacity contracts with DC/telco hosts Utilisation + optimisation; SLOs, cost per request Low (rent GPUs first)
    B: AppCo (Regulated Vertical Apps) 1-2 workflow-native apps (contact centre QA + compliance for banking; document intelligence for public procurement) Per seat / per interaction + compliance add-on Low-medium
    C: Infra SPV (GPU Pods; later DC) -- Optional Capex entity for GPU pods, optionally large DC. For mezzanine/project finance, offtake contracts, state incentives MW delivered, contracted utilisation, PUE Very high
