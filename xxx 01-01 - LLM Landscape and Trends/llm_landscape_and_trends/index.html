    Opening Briefing¶

    The defining shift of the past year is not a new architecture but a change in what counts as the artifact. Between February 2025 and February 2026, no fundamentally novel backbone displaced the autoregressive decoder-only transformer. Instead, frontier development converged on four high-leverage ideas layered on top of it — controllable thinking modes, mixture-of-experts sparsity, million-plus-token context windows, and system-level agent integration — and in doing so moved the unit of competitive advantage from the model to the system: model plus search plus verification plus tools plus memory plus orchestration. The implication is structural: the moat now lives in the inference and systems layer, not in pre-training scale.

    A counter-intuitive pricing trend has also emerged: newer, smaller models are not always cheaper. GPT-5.4 nano launched at $0.2/$1.25 per 1M input/output tokens — a 4x increase over old GPT-5 nano ($0.05/$0.4) — while Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite at $0.25/$1.50 achieves 75% accuracy at 96.1% cost savings versus Gemini 3.1 Pro (over 10k calls: $11.41 vs $292) and runs 3.8x faster (AINews, Mar 2026). The floor is rising because vendors are baking more post-training quality into small form factors, and users are willing to pay for it. At the same time, Chinese entrants undercut on absolute price: MiniMax M2.7 at $0.30/$1.20 matches GLM-5 (Reasoning) on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 50 at less than one-third GLM-5's cost, and Xiaomi MiMo-V2-Pro offers 1M context at $1/$3 with stronger token efficiency and lower hallucination than peers (Artificial Analysis, Mar 2026).

    The million-token context race illustrates the convergence vividly. Google shipped 1M context first with Gemini in February 2024, but by March 2026 every frontier lab had followed: OpenAI GA'd its own 1M context the first week of March, and days later Anthropic made Opus 4.6 with 1M context the default for Max, Team, and Enterprise users, removing the API's long-context surcharge, dropping the beta header requirement, and expanding media limits to 600 images or PDF pages per request (@_catwu, @alexalbert__, Latent Space AINews, Mar 2026). Opus 4.6 posted 78.3% on MRCR v2 at 1M tokens, a new long-context high watermark that underscored how the competitive axis has shifted from merely offering a large window to demonstrating reliable recall and reasoning across it.

    The clearest evidence is the independent convergence on hybrid reasoning controls. OpenAI's GPT-5 routes between fast and thinking sub-models; Gemini 2.5 Flash exposes a controllable thinking budget; Claude Sonnet 4.6 introduces an effort parameter; Qwen3 integrates thinking and non-thinking modes; gpt-oss trains discrete low/medium/high reasoning levels with smooth test-time scaling. Five organizations, zero coordination, one design pattern. This is not coincidence — it reflects a shared recognition that inference-time compute is the new scaling axis, one that is selective and task-aware rather than fixed and sunk. Meanwhile, MoE sparsity became the default scaling direction across both closed and open-weight families: Llama 4 activates 17B of 109–400B total parameters, gpt-oss-120b fits on a single 80 GB GPU via MXFP4 quantization, and Gemini 2.5 uses dynamic sparse routing. The old narrative of "just scale dense parameters" is now a historical footnote.

    The GPT-5.4 release (Mar 5, 2026) crystallized a new competitive reality: "there is no single best frontier model anymore" (Louie Peters, Towards AI, Mar 2026). GPT-5.4 leads ProofBench, IOI, and Vibe Code Bench; Gemini 3.1 Pro leads LegalBench, GPQA, MMLU Pro, LiveCodeBench, and Terminal-Bench 2.0; Claude Opus 4.6 leads SWE-bench; Claude Sonnet 4.6 leads the Vals composite and Finance Agent. On aggregate indices the margins are razor-thin: GPT-5.4 ties Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview at 57 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, and on LiveBench GPT-5.4 Thinking xHigh scores 80.28 vs Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview 79.93 (Artificial Analysis, Mar 2026; Louie Peters, Towards AI, Mar 2026). Google DeepMind's Gemma 4 31B dense model (Apr 2026) further sharpened this picture: it ties with Kimi K2.5 (744B-A40B) and Z.ai GLM-5 (1T-A32B) on Arena open-model rankings despite having a fraction of their total parameters, scoring GPQA Diamond 85.7% and ranking #1 among US open models — evidence that architectural efficiency and training recipe now dominate over raw parameter scale (Google DeepMind, AINews, Apr 2026). The benchmark grid has splintered — model selection is now a per-task decision, not a per-vendor one. On domain-specific benchmarks GPT-5.4 posted striking gains: GDPval 83.0% (vs 70.9% GPT-5.2, vs 12.4% GPT-4o ~18 months earlier), OSWorld-Verified 75.0% (surpassing the human baseline of 72.4% and nearly doubling GPT-5.2's 47.3%), Harvey BigLaw Bench 91%, Mainstay property-tax portal automation at 95% first-try / 100% within 3 tries / 3x faster / 70% fewer tokens across ~30,000 HOA portals, internal spreadsheet modeling 87.3% (vs 68.4% GPT-5.2), with 33% fewer false claims and 18% fewer error-containing responses vs GPT-5.2 (Louie Peters, Towards AI, Mar 2026).

    What these observations mean together is a second-order consequence that practitioners must internalize: intelligence is becoming elastic at inference time. A single model family, equipped with adjustable effort, verifiable-reward RL for correctness-driven improvement, and tool-mediated execution, can serve both a sub-second classification and a multi-minute agentic research workflow — the cost and quality knob is turned per request, not per deployment. This makes the evaluation regime, the tool-permissioning layer, and the orchestration logic at least as load-bearing as the weights themselves.

    This document serves as the index and strategic overview of the LLM landscape. It traces the paradigm shift from scale to systems, catalogs model releases, and provides practitioner recommendations. For technical depth on any topic, follow the deep-dive links above.

    The Paradigm Shift: Scale to Systems¶

    Old Center of Gravity New Center of Gravity
    Larger models, more data, more training compute, benchmark chasing via scale Better post-training, better inference-time behavior, reasoning traces, verification, system-level orchestration

    The moat is shifting from the base model to the inference and systems layer. Optimization target: how much computation you spend after the prompt arrives, whether the model can test/revise/compare/verify, whether it can route work to tools/sub-agents, and whether outputs are auditable and grounded.

    Highest-Value Themes (2025-2026)¶

    1. Verifiable-reward RL — scalable correctness-driven improvement (code, math). → Reasoning & Inference Scaling
    2. Inference-time scaling — compute shifts from training-only to selective runtime search. → Reasoning & Inference Scaling
    3. Bounded self-refinement — generate, critique, revise as default pattern. → Post-Training & Inference-Time Techniques
    4. Verifier-mediated selection — outputs compete under checks, not style. → Reasoning & Inference Scaling
    5. Multi-agent coordination as governance — specialist roles, quorum, evidence-weighted consensus. → Agentic Systems
    6. Architecture shifts serving reasoning economics — MoE, sparse attention, latent-state efficiency. → LLM Architectures

    Adoption & Traction Analysis¶

    Clearly Gained Adoption¶

    1. Hybrid reasoning controls — table stakes across OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, open-weight families
    2. MoE and sparsity — moved from "researchy" to mainstream default scaling direction
    3. Agentic tool ecosystems — productised with explicit pricing and safety evaluations
    4. Open-weight strategic parity — gpt-oss (Apache 2.0), Llama 4, Qwen3, and now Gemma 4 (Apache 2.0, Apr 2026 — a major licensing upgrade from prior restrictive Gemma terms) make "API vs self-hosted" an engineering trade, not capability cliff. Gemma 3 had already reached 400M downloads and 100K variants before the Gemma 4 launch, demonstrating the commercial appetite for open-weight models with permissive licensing
    5. Regional/language-specialist open models — Sarvam AI Sarvam-30B and Sarvam-105B trained from scratch for Indian languages with competitive global benchmarks; distributed via HuggingFace and AIKosh with SGLang launch support and expected vLLM integration. Chinese labs continue to ship competitive reasoning models: Xiaomi MiMo-V2-Pro (API-only, Intelligence Index 49, 1M context, GDPval-AA Elo 1426) follows the open-weight MiMo-V2-Flash (309B total / 15B active, MIT license), while MiniMax M2.7 (Intelligence Index 50, 56.22% SWE-Pro, 57.0% Terminal Bench 2, 97% skill adherence across 40+ skills) reaches Sonnet 4.6 parity in OpenClaw at roughly one-third the cost of comparable models (Artificial Analysis, Mar 2026; AINews, Mar 2026). However, Alibaba is pulling back from open-source: Qwen Image 2.0 was reclassified from "Open-Source" to "Release" on the Qwen research page, reportedly driven by CEO dissatisfaction with revenue from open models. Key engineers have departed, suggesting a strategic pivot that may reduce the flow of competitive open-weight models from Alibaba going forward (Latent Space AINews, Mar 2026)
    6. Hardware-model vertical integration — Nvidia announced a $26B, five-year commitment to develop open-weight AI models optimized for its own hardware (NVFP4 precision, H100 clusters), applying the classic "commoditize your product's complement" strategy: free models locked to CUDA make it harder for competitors to justify alternative chip designs, reinforcing Nvidia's dominance as the default inference target even as it nominally competes with closed-model providers like OpenAI and Anthropic whose profit center is model access, not silicon (Latent.Space / r/LocalLlama, Mar 2026). The first concrete artifact is NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Ultra Base, a ~500B-parameter open base model claiming 5x efficiency on GB200 NVL72 and "best open base model" status — though benchmarks compare against GLM and Kimi K2 base models (not instruct/reasoning finetunes), drawing community skepticism given Kimi K2 is ~8 months old (AINews, Mar 2026). Architecturally, Nemotron 3 drew attention for mixing Transformer + Mamba 2, MoE/LatentMoE, multi-token prediction, and NVFP4 precision in service of lower inference costs and long-context agent workloads — with Tri Dao noting nonlinear RNN layers appear to add something distinct from attention and linear SSMs (AINews, Mar 2026)
    7. AI labs acquiring developer tooling as strategic moat — every major lab serious about developers has now bought their own devtools: OpenAI acquired Astral (uv, ruff, ty — foundational Python tooling) for the Codex team, Anthropic acquired Bun (Dec 2025), and Google DeepMind acquired the Antigravity team (Jul 2025, now powering AI Studio's coding agent). OpenAI's acquisition came alongside Fidji Simo explicitly dropping "side quests" like Shopping (Walmart partnership reporting ~1/3 conversion of click-outs) and prioritizing Enterprise (Frontier Alliances) and Coding (Astral), with plans to unify ChatGPT and Codex into a single "superapp." The pattern is clear: the labs view ownership of foundational developer infrastructure as a platform moat, not just a product feature (@charliermarsh, @gdb, Latent Space AINews, Mar 2026)
    8. Continued pretraining before RL as competitive lever — Cursor's Composer 2 explicitly attributed its quality gains to its first continued pretraining run feeding a stronger base into RL, a pattern researchers argue will become standard for specialized models. Composer 2 launched at $0.50/M input and $2.50/M output, scoring 61.3 on CursorBench, 61.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, and 73.7 on SWE-bench Multilingual, with a ~40-person team focused exclusively on software engineering tasks. The launch included Glass, an early alpha agent-native UI that commentary suggests the industry will converge on (@cursor_ai, @kimmonismus, @theo, Mar 2026). Relatedly, the "Finetuner's Fallacy" (@pratyushmaini) shows that early training data leaves a durable imprint on model representations that later finetuning struggles to undo — implying pre-training data composition matters more than most practitioners assume. Architecture exploration beyond standard transformers also remains lively: M²RNN (@MayankMish98) revisits non-linear recurrence with matrix-valued states for scalable language modeling (AINews, Mar 2026)

    Declined or Niche¶

    • Pure encoder-decoder seq2seq for general assistants (T5Gemma shows research interest, not deployment reality)
    • "Just scale dense parameters" narrative — shifted to sparsity, routing, inference-time controls

    Practitioner Recommendations¶

    Model Selection Heuristics¶

    1. Start from the system shape, not the model. Freshness/provenance → RAG + citations. Action-taking → agent + tool sandboxing. Latency → quantisation/speculative decoding. Then pick base model.
    2. Use thinking budget as primary quality knob. Single family with adjustable effort reduces regressions and simplifies evaluation.
    3. Prefer MoE/open-weight when you control infra and throughput dominates.

    Modern Default Pipeline (2026)¶

    • Retriever: encoder-only embeddings + vector store + lightweight reranker
    • Generator: hybrid reasoning model with effort control
    • Tools: web search / file search / code execution / domain APIs with permissioning and audit
    • Safety: input filtering + output validation + prompt-injection defences (first-class red-team target)
    • Evaluation: task-specific regression suite + agent trajectory evals (not static QA scores)

    Risks & Ethical Considerations¶

    • Open-weight safety externalisation: once weights released, safety mitigations cannot be revoked; responsibility shifts to deployers
    • Prompt injection in agentic systems: treat as engineering problem (tool permissioning, content isolation, instruction hierarchy)
    • Chain-of-thought handling: log reasoning summaries for debugging, keep raw traces restricted
    • Training data provenance: document data rights, opt-in policies, retention boundaries for regulated deployments
    • Evaluation contamination: benchmark wins should be discounted unless reproducible on private data
    • Recursive self-improvement and oversight erosion: Claude now writes 70-90% of the code for future Anthropic models, and Evan Hubinger (Anthropic) suggests fully automated AI research could be imminent, potentially within a year. As models write their own code, humans shift from creators to reviewers, complicating oversight because reviewers lack intuitive understanding of AI-generated decisions. Claude 3.7 Sonnet's release was delayed 10 days due to safety concerns, illustrating the tension between rapid advancement and safety protocols. Dario Amodei warns AI could displace half of entry-level white-collar jobs within 5 years. Infrastructure timeline compounds the urgency: by Q3 2027, new "Rubin Ultra" hardware is expected, with bespoke hardware exponentially increasing processing power (Latent.Space / r/Singularity, Mar 2026). MiniMax M2.7 pushes this boundary further: MiniMax positioned it as the first model that "deeply participated in its own evolution," with an internal harness that recursively improved itself — collecting feedback, building eval sets, and iterating on skills/MCP, memory, and architecture (AINews, Mar 2026). The pattern of models contributing to their own training pipelines is no longer a one-lab curiosity. Karpathy's autoresearch (Mar 2026) demonstrated this concretely: an LLM agent autonomously discovered ~20 additive improvements to a nanochat training loop in 2 days, reducing "Time to GPT-2" from 2.02h to 1.80h (~11%), with changes transferring from a depth-12 proxy to a depth-24 model (Andrej Karpathy, Mar 2026). OpenAI's GPT-5.3-Codex was described as the "first model instrumental in creating itself" — early versions were used to debug training, manage deployment, and diagnose evals. The GPT-5.4 system card states the model does not meet the "High" self-improvement threshold (defined as ~performant mid-career research engineer), but the economically useful threshold is much lower — "AI is becoming a closed-loop improver of its own stack" (Louie Peters, Towards AI, Mar 2026). OpenAI's release cadence underscores the acceleration: GPT-5.2 (Dec 2025), GPT-5.3-Codex (Feb 5), Codex-Spark (Feb 12), GPT-5.3 Instant (Mar 3), GPT-5.4 (Mar 5) — "monthly releases are here" (OpenAI staff) (Louie Peters, Towards AI, Mar 2026)
