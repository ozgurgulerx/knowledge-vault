    Supplement Master Reference — Evidence, Dosing, and Monitoring¶

    Opening Briefing¶

    This is the consolidated reference table for all supplements discussed across health domains. The guiding principles: food-first for nutrients, supplement-first only for specific mechanisms (creatine for phosphocreatine, viscous fibre for bile acid binding, plant sterols for cholesterol absorption competition). Change one thing every 2–6 weeks, re-check symptoms + performance + key biomarkers, then keep/kill. Prefer single-ingredient products with clear dosing. Avoid proprietary blends.

    Lean-stack philosophy: ACR conditionally recommends food-first approaches for rheumatoid arthritis management, and major glaucoma guidance centers on IOP control and structured follow-up rather than supplement stacks (PMC 10947582). Do not add new supplements — magnesium, nicotinamide, lutein "for eye protection," or otherwise — without a clear, specific indication backed by your clinician. The goal is the smallest effective stack, not the largest plausible one.

    Evidence Strength Key¶

    • Strong = consistent RCT/meta support for the stated outcome and widely used in practice
    • Moderate = meta/RCT support but heterogeneity/short trials or not guideline-endorsed
    • Limited = small/inconsistent studies
    • Medical-supervision = meaningful safety/regulatory issues requiring clinician monitoring

    Core Stack (Start Here)¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Primary Target Key Risks Monitor
    Creatine monohydrate 3–5 g daily (no loading needed) Strong (strength/lean mass), Moderate (cognition) Strength, body composition, possible cognitive benefit under stress/sleep loss Water retention (weight), GI upset; serum creatinine may rise (interpret labs carefully) 8–12 weeks alongside progressive strength plan; check kidney function if factors present
    Psyllium husk (viscous fibre) 5–10 g, 1–2×/day with plenty of water Moderate–Strong (lipids) LDL/non-HDL reduction; appetite support; glycaemic response Bloating; can reduce absorption of some meds/supplements if taken together Trial 6–8 weeks; separate from meds by ~2 hours; re-check lipids
    Oat/barley β-glucan ~3 g/day Moderate (lipids) LDL reduction with high adherence; easy breakfast anchor GI gas if ramped fast Add daily oats/barley for 6–8 weeks; re-check lipids

    General Health¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Primary Target Key Risks Monitor
    Vitamin D3 600–2,000 IU/day maintenance (adults) Strong (bone/calcium); Mixed (extra-skeletal) Keep at a sane maintenance dose — not megadoses. The adult tolerable upper intake level is 4,000 IU/day. Toxicity from supplement overuse can cause hypercalcemia and hypercalciuria and, in extreme cases, renal failure. Do not add routine calcium supplementation unless there is a specific bone-related indication (ODS NIH Vitamin D fact sheet). Hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria at sustained high doses; kidney stones; possible interaction with thiazide diuretics Check 25(OH)D level at baseline; re-check after 3 months; adjust to maintain sufficiency range

    Lipid-Targeted Supplements¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Primary Target Key Risks Monitor
    Plant sterols/stanols 1.5–2.4 g/day with meals (split doses) Moderate (LDL) 6–12% LDL lowering; best combined with diet changes Sitosterolaemia (rare); possible fat-soluble vitamin absorption issues at high intakes; "once daily without meal" less effective 6–8 weeks; take with meals; re-check non-HDL/LDL
    Omega-3 (EPA+DHA) 1–2 g/day general; ~4 g EPA/DHA for TG lowering (often prescription-level) Strong (TG lowering), Mixed (hard CV outcomes) TG reduction; may help inflammation/soreness modestly. Keep current dose if it clearly helps or if fish intake is low, but do not escalate "just because more seems anti-inflammatory" — the current typical dose is well below the combined EPA+DHA ceiling that regulators consider generally safe (~3 g/day supplemental per FDA), so there is no urgency to push higher (ODS NIH Omega-3 fact sheet). Bleeding risk mainly theoretical at typical doses; GI reflux; quality variability Use fish 2×/week first; supplement if needed; choose verified brand; re-check TG
    Berberine 500 mg 2×/day with meals Moderate (lipids/insulin markers) LDL/TG and glycaemic improvements (esp. if insulin resistance) GI upset; drug interactions (CYP/P-gp); avoid in pregnancy; product quality variability Trial only if diet/exercise insufficient and insulin resistance present; monitor LFT, glucose, and meds list
    Red yeast rice (monacolin K) Variable; often "statin-like" exposure Effective but medical-supervision LDL lowering (can be substantial) Myopathy/rhabdo risk; liver enzyme elevation; major interactions; monacolin variability + citrinin contamination; EU safety concerns/regulatory restrictions Clinician-supervised only with LFT/CK monitoring; avoid combining with statins

    Sexual Function / Vascular Support¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Primary Target Key Risks Monitor
    L-citrulline ~1.5 g/day (ED); 3–6 g/day (performance) Limited–Moderate (mild ED), Moderate (exercise blood-flow) ↑ arginine/NO → endothelial function Hypotension if combined with nitrates/PDE5 inhibitors or BP meds; GI upset 4–8 week trial; track morning erections/IIEF as proxy + training response
    L-arginine Variable Limited–Moderate (mild–moderate ED) Direct NO precursor Hypotension interactions Watch BP if on meds
    Panax ginseng 0.8–3 g/day extract Moderate (ED scores, modest) Endothelial/NO + central effects Interacts with warfarin/antiplatelets; insomnia/jitteriness; quality variability 8–12 week trial; stop if insomnia/palpitations

    Stress, Sleep, and Recovery¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Primary Target Key Risks Monitor
    Ashwagandha 300–600 mg/day standardised extract Moderate (stress/anxiety markers) Stress resilience; potential downstream libido/energy benefit GI upset; caution with thyroid issues; rare liver injury reports 8-week trial with clear endpoints (sleep, HRV trend, perceived stress)
    Magnesium (glycinate) 200–400 mg elemental/day (split) Limited–Moderate (sleep quality; mixed evidence) Small sleep quality gains if deficient or high stress Diarrhoea (oxide/citrate more than glycinate); interacts with tetracyclines/quinolones/levothyroxine 4–6 week trial; stop if GI issues; avoid with significant kidney disease
    Melatonin Low dose, short-term Mixed (chronic insomnia in adults) Circadian timing tool Not a lifelong sedative; evidence for chronic use is limited Use as timing aid, not sleep medication

    Joint / Inflammation¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Primary Target Key Risks Monitor
    Curcumin (high-bioavailability) 500–1,000 mg/day Moderate–Limited (OA pain/function) Symptom relief if joint pain emerges; may reduce NSAID need. If curcumin feels helpful, keep it at the lowest effective dose — don't assume more is better. Official safety summaries mostly flag GI side effects and supplement-quality variability rather than proving long-term extra benefit at higher intakes. ACR's bias is food first: turmeric in meals is fine, but don't expand into a larger herb stack (NCCIH Turmeric). GI upset; interacts with anticoagulants/antiplatelets; gallbladder issues 8–12 week trial tracking symptom scores; stop if bruising or GI issues
    Collagen peptides 5–10 g/day Moderate (OA pain/function; evolving) Possible pain/function improvements, especially early OA or activity-related pain Generally well tolerated; quality varies 3–6 month trial anchored to strength + load management

    Glaucoma-Adjacent Supplements (Experimental / Specialist-Led)¶

    Supplement Evidence Status
    Nicotinamide (vitamin B3) Crossover RCT showed improved inner retinal function in glaucoma Experimental, specialist-led — promising but not settled; dosing can be high
    Citicoline Mixed; systematic review = insufficient evidence for slowing progression Research-adjacent; not routine OTC
    Ginkgo biloba Small trials; systematic review = limited/heterogeneous Bleeding/drug interaction concerns; not recommended as default

    Protein (Supplemental)¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Notes
    Whey/casein protein Dose = to reach daily protein target (~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day) Strong Practical tool when food alone is insufficient. GI intolerance (lactose); some lipid worsening possible via added saturated fat in some products. Track total protein + satiety

    Decision Rules¶

    1. Get baseline bloods first (lipids, ApoB, Lp(a), HbA1c, BP, eye exam) — don't supplement blind
    2. Start minimal: Creatine + psyllium/oats daily. Add from there based on data
    3. One change every 2–6 weeks, then re-assess
    4. If QRISK3 ≥10% or lipids at urgent thresholds, don't rely on supplements alone — discuss statins
    5. Defer berberine/RYR until lipid phenotype is confirmed and diet/exercise have been trialled
    6. Never combine red yeast rice with statins without medical supervision
    7. Avoid proprietary "male enhancement" blends — FDA warns about hidden drug ingredients
