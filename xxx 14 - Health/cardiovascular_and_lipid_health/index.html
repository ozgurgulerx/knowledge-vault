    Cardiovascular Health, Lipid Management, and ASCVD Risk¶

    Opening Briefing¶

    "Elevated blood lipids" is not one problem — it can be primarily LDL/ApoB-driven (atherogenic particle number), triglyceride-driven, or mixed. The actions overlap, but the prioritisation and "supplement ROI" change substantially depending on which phenotype dominates. The foundational move is getting the numbers: lipid fractions + ApoB + Lp(a) + BP + HbA1c, then building a strategy around what the data shows. Without ApoB and Lp(a), you're optimising blind.

    Baseline Risk Assessment¶

    Primary Risks¶

    • ASCVD risk may be under-estimated if you have high ApoB discordance or high triglycerides; the presence of ED (if present/episodic) can be a risk-enhancing marker in some frameworks
    • Standard lipid panel is insufficient — ApoB is the better predictor of atherogenic particle burden than LDL-C alone. Lp(a) should be measured at least once in adulthood (it's genetically determined and largely unmodifiable by lifestyle, but it changes risk stratification)

    What to Measure and When¶

    Baseline now (repeat in ~8–12 weeks after interventions):

    • Lipids: TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, TG, non-HDL; add ApoB if available
    • Lp(a) — at least once; re-check only if there's a specific reason
    • BP (home averages)
    • HbA1c (or fasting glucose), plus liver enzymes and kidney function if considering berberine/RYR or if creatine labs are a concern
    • Eye exam: IOP + optic nerve/fields baseline if glaucoma risk is meaningful

    Ongoing cadence: Lipids at least annually; sooner after major diet/supplement changes or after statin initiation.

    When Medication Should Enter the Conversation¶

    If 10-year QRISK3 is ≥10%, UK guidance recommends offering atorvastatin 20 mg for primary prevention. Also consider statins if risk is underestimated or you prefer treatment despite lower calculated risk. This matters because OTC interventions rarely match statin effect sizes for LDL/ApoB.

    Urgent thresholds: Total cholesterol >9.0 mmol/L or non-HDL >7.5 mmol/L → specialist assessment recommended even without family history. TG >10 mmol/L → repeat fasting promptly; TG >20 mmol/L not explained by alcohol/glycaemia → urgent specialist review (pancreatitis risk pathway).

    Dietary Strategy — Mediterranean + Portfolio Hybrid¶

    The North Star¶

    A Mediterranean base (best evidence for cardiometabolic outcomes) plus Portfolio elements (best evidence density for LDL lowering via specific foods). This is the most evidence-dense nutrition approach for lipids and overall cardiovascular outcomes (PREDIMED, NEJM).

    Macronutrients¶

    For a 48-year-old male running ~40 km/week:

    • Protein: Target ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day, biased higher during fat-loss phases and when adding strength training volume. Distribute reasonably across meals
    • Carbs (periodised): Match carb intake to training demand — higher on long/quality run days, lower on easy/rest days. This preserves performance while allowing leanness phases without chronic glycogen depletion
    • Fats: Prioritise unsaturated fats (olive oil, nuts, seeds, oily fish). Keep saturated fat low enough that ApoB/LDL improves — confirm via labs

    What to Increase (High Impact)¶

    Viscous fibre / "lipid binders" daily — Aim for a daily combo of oats/barley (β-glucan), legumes, chia/flax, and optionally psyllium. This is one of the most reliable "non-drug" LDL levers.

    Plant-forward proteins — Swap some red-meat meals for lentils/beans, soy/tofu/tempeh, nuts, fish, and poultry. Evidence suggests replacing red meat with high-quality plant proteins is more consistently favourable for LDL/TC than replacement with refined carbs.

    Extra-virgin olive oil + nuts — Mediterranean interventions supplemented with olive oil or nuts reduced major cardiovascular events in high-risk populations (PREDIMED). Even if your absolute baseline risk differs, the mechanism set (fat quality, polyphenols, diet displacement) is directly relevant.

    What to Decrease (Targeted, Not Dogmatic)¶

    Processed meat: Minimise — highest consistency of harm signals across cardiometabolic outcomes; also tends to raise salt intake.

    Red meat guidance (practical, UK-compatible):

    • If routinely exceeding ~70 g/day cooked weight of red + processed meat, cutting down is recommended per UK public health guidance
    • For lipids specifically: the key is often saturated fat and what you replace meat with — plant protein substitution is more consistently favourable than refined carb substitution
    • The saturated fat evidence base is more nuanced than public messaging suggests; use your biomarkers (ApoB/LDL/non-HDL) as the deciding instrument rather than ideology

    Alcohol/smoking: Both are negative multipliers for lipids, recovery, erections, and eye health. If you're already mostly avoiding, maintain.

    Meal Timing for Performance + Leanness¶

    • Pre-run (quality/long): Carbs + some protein, low fibre/fat to reduce GI issues
    • Post-run (especially after quality): Protein + carbs within a couple of hours if doing frequent sessions; improves glycogen restoration and supports adaptation
    • For leanness: Keep most carbs around training, make the rest of the day more protein + veg + unsaturated fat dominant ("carb steering") — preserves training quality while reducing passive overeating

    Supplement Stack for Lipid Management¶

    Guiding Principles¶

    1. Food-first for nutrients; supplement-first for specific mechanisms (e.g., creatine for phosphocreatine, viscous fibre for bile acid binding, plant sterols for cholesterol absorption competition)
    2. Change one thing every 2–6 weeks, re-check (symptoms + performance + key biomarkers), then keep/kill
    3. Prefer single-ingredient products with clear dosing. Avoid proprietary blends, especially "sexual enhancement/energy" products due to frequent hidden PDE5 inhibitors or other drugs (FDA warnings)
    4. Be extra conservative with anything statin-like (e.g., red yeast rice) because of adverse effect profiles and EU safety/regulatory actions on monacolins

    The Evidence-Based Stack¶

    Supplement Dose Evidence Key Notes
    Psyllium husk 5–10 g, 1–2×/day with water Moderate–Strong (lipids) LDL/non-HDL reduction; appetite support. Separate from meds by ~2 hours
    Oat/barley β-glucan ~3 g/day Moderate (lipids) LDL reduction with high adherence; easy breakfast anchor. Trial 6–8 weeks
    Plant sterols/stanols 1.5–2.4 g/day with meals (split doses) Moderate (LDL) Typically 6–12% LDL lowering in trials; best combined with diet changes. Possible fat-soluble vitamin absorption issues at high intakes
    Omega-3 (EPA+DHA) 1–2 g/day general; ~4 g EPA/DHA for TG lowering Strong (TG), Mixed (hard CV outcomes) Meaningful TG reduction at higher dose; use fish 2×/week first; choose verified brand
    Berberine 500 mg 2×/day with meals Moderate (lipids/insulin) Potential LDL/TG and glycaemic improvements (especially if insulin resistance present). Drug interactions (CYP/P-gp); avoid in pregnancy; product quality varies. Trial only if diet/exercise insufficient
    Red yeast rice Variable (statin-like exposure) Effective but medical-supervision LDL lowering can be substantial. Myopathy/rhabdo risk; liver enzyme elevation; monacolin variability + citrinin contamination; EU safety concerns. Clinician-supervised only; avoid combining with statins

    Supplements NOT to Default To¶

    • Glucosamine for lipids/joints: weak evidence; NICE does not recommend for OA management
    • Generic "male enhancement" blends: FDA reports many are adulterated with hidden drug ingredients (sildenafil/tadalafil), creating dangerous interaction risk with nitrates and unreliable dosing

    Monitoring and Red Flags¶

    When Lipids Demand Escalation¶

    • Total cholesterol >9.0 mmol/L or non-HDL >7.5 mmol/L → specialist assessment even without family history
    • TG >10 mmol/L → repeat fasting promptly
    • TG >20 mmol/L not explained by alcohol/glycaemia → urgent specialist review (pancreatitis risk)
    • QRISK3 ≥10% → discuss statin initiation

    Cardiac Red Flags — Don't Optimise Through These¶

    • Chest pain, syncope, disproportionate breathlessness, or ED plus exercise intolerance → urgent cardiovascular evaluation
    • Supplements causing muscle pain/weakness + dark urine (especially on red yeast rice or if combining lipid agents) → stop and seek care
