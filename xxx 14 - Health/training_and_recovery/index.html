    Training, Recovery, and Joint Health¶

    Opening Briefing¶

    At ~40 km/week running with elevated lipids, glaucoma risk, and arthritis concerns, the highest-yield training strategy is not more volume — it's quality distribution, strength training, and recovery. Adults should include muscle-strengthening activities at least 2 days/week (WHO). The aerobic base is already strong; strength is likely under-dosed. Strength training is a cornerstone for function and symptom control in OA, is plausibly protective via improved mechanics and load tolerance, and is non-negotiable at 48.

    Weekly Running Structure¶

    A template that works well at ~40 km/week volume:

    • 2–3 easy runs (Zone 2 / conversational)
    • 1 long run (progressively 75–120 min depending on current tolerance)
    • 1 quality session (alternate weekly between tempo/threshold and VO₂ intervals)
    • 1 optional recovery jog or cross-train (bike/row) if stress/sleep debt is high

    Why: VO₂max and threshold improve best when intensity is structured; most joint issues come from "too much moderate-hard" and from spikes in load.

    Strength Training (Non-Negotiable at 48)¶

    Resistance training improves pain/function in OA populations and is one of the best "prehab" moves even before symptoms, because it improves strength, tendon capacity, and movement tolerance.

    Programme Design (2–3 sessions/week, 45–60 min)¶

    • Lower body: Squat pattern (front/goblet), hinge (RDL), split squat/step-ups, calf raises
    • Upper body: Horizontal push/pull (bench/push-ups + rows), vertical push/pull (overhead press + pull-down/pull-ups)
    • Joint-stability add-ons: Hip abduction (glute med), hamstring eccentrics, rotator cuff, tibialis raises
    • Core: Anti-rotation + anti-extension (Pallof press, dead bug variations)

    Progression: Stay ~2–3 reps in reserve most sets; add load/reps slowly. The goal is repeatability, not weekly PRs.

    Glaucoma-safe strength notes: For glaucoma-susceptible individuals, keep sessions moderate — full-body work at sub-max loads, no ego lifting. Use continuous breathing (exhale on effort) and avoid prolonged Valsalva manoeuvres; heavy static effort, breath-holding, and inverted or head-down positions can transiently raise intraocular pressure. That means no long head-down yoga holds and no max-effort straining. The practical weekly template accommodates this well: keep runs + 2 x 30–45 min strength sessions + 1 short mobility session on a non-run day (PMC 10947582).

    HIIT Without Overreaching¶

    Add one HIIT-style session/week if sleep/stress is stable; otherwise keep it every 10–14 days. Practical options:

    • 6×2 min hard (but controlled) with 2–3 min easy recoveries
    • 4×4 min at high but sustainable intensity

    Pick what you recover from.

    Recovery and Injury Prevention¶

    Load Management¶

    Avoid abrupt weekly mileage or intensity jumps; connective tissue lags behind cardio fitness. The 10% rule is a rough heuristic — the real principle is don't spike what you can ramp.

    Sleep Debt Rule¶

    If you've had <6–6.5 h sleep for 2 nights, treat your next session as easy or strength-only. Sleep loss is one of the strongest predictors of "everything feels harder," including erectile function.

    Footwear and Surfaces¶

    Rotate shoes; avoid doing all quality work on cambered/hard surfaces (common runner knee and Achilles triggers).

    Workday Movement Breaks¶

    Prolonged sitting is an independent cardiometabolic risk factor, but even very short activity breaks make a measurable difference. NIH data shows that 1–5 minute bouts of movement during sitting are beneficial, and a Columbia crossover study found the best acute cardiometabolic result from 5 minutes of walking every 30 minutes of prolonged sitting (NIH Research Matters). A practical version: aim for 3–5 minutes of walking every 30–60 minutes during desk blocks, take calls while walking when possible, and choose stairs over lifts. A standing desk is a useful transition tool — it reduces static sitting time — but it is not a substitute for actual movement breaks.

    Glaucoma-Aware Training Modifications¶

    • Avoid breath-holding under heavy strain (Valsalva) and avoid sustained head-down inversion work if you have known glaucoma/OHT or strong risk. These can acutely raise intraocular pressure. Use exhale-on-effort, submax loads, and avoid inversion holds
    • Aerobic exercise can reduce IOP transiently and is generally supportive as part of healthy lifestyle — just don't assume it replaces eye monitoring

    Arthritis Risk Management¶

    • Recreational running is not automatically an OA sentence — injury history, total load, and strength are the big levers
    • Strength training is a cornerstone for function and symptom control in OA and is plausibly protective via improved mechanics and load tolerance
    • If you develop persistent swollen/hot joints, prolonged morning stiffness, or systemic symptoms, treat as possible inflammatory arthritis and get assessed — don't self-manage with supplements alone (NICE OA guidance)

    Rheumatoid Arthritis — Don't Manage by Symptoms Alone¶

    Even when feeling good and not flaring, keep periodic rheumatology follow-up. ACR treatment guidance follows a treat-to-target approach with formal disease-activity monitoring (composite scores, labs), not symptom-only self-assessment (PMC 9273041). Tapering medication assumes sustained, objectively confirmed remission and generally retains at least one DMARD rather than stopping everything because things "feel fine." ACR also strongly recommends consistent exercise — both aerobic and resistance training — for RA, reinforcing that the strength-training habit serves double duty for joint protection and disease management (PMC 10947582).

    OTC for Joint Symptoms¶

    If osteoarthritis-type pain emerges, NICE-aligned UK practice prioritises: topical NSAIDs first, then oral NSAIDs only when needed (lowest effective dose, shortest duration), with gastroprotection considered. Paracetamol is not routinely recommended for OA (limited benefit). Glucosamine is not recommended (weak evidence).

    Supplement options with some evidence:

    • Curcumin (high-bioavailability extract, 500–1,000 mg/day): Moderate–Limited evidence for OA pain/function; may reduce NSAID need in some. Interacts with anticoagulants/antiplatelets; GI and gallbladder issues possible. 8–12 week trial tracking symptom scores
    • Collagen peptides (5–10 g/day): Moderate evidence for OA pain/function (evidence evolving); generally well tolerated. 3–6 month trial anchored to strength + load management

    Practical Weekly Schedule¶

    Assumptions: ~40 km/week running, 2 strength sessions + one optional, Mediterranean/Portfolio diet, minimal supplement stack.

    Day Training Meals Focus Supplements
    Mon Easy run 8–10 km + mobility 10 min High fibre day: oats + berries; legumes at lunch; oily fish dinner Creatine; fibre (psyllium or oats); omega-3 only if using
    Tue Strength A (45–60 min) + optional 20 min easy jog Higher protein + carbs around lift (rice/potatoes + lean protein) Creatine; citrulline (optional)
    Wed Quality run (tempo or intervals) 8–12 km incl. work Carbs up (fruit + grains) + lean protein; low fibre pre-run Creatine; consider electrolytes if heavy sweat
    Thu Easy run 6–8 km or cross-train + short core Portfolio emphasis: soy/tofu/beans + nuts; olive oil Creatine; fibre
    Fri Strength B (45–60 min) Protein-forward; veg-heavy; moderate carbs Creatine; magnesium at night if trialling
    Sat Long run 12–18 km easy Carbs up; post-run protein + carbs within 2 h Creatine; fibre later (not pre-run)
    Sun Rest or very easy 5–6 km + walk "Reset" day: big salad + legumes; limit red meat Creatine; fibre

    Minimal Supplement Stack (Start Here)¶

    • Creatine 3–5 g daily
    • Psyllium (or oats/β-glucan) daily
    • Optional: magnesium at night only if sleep/stress issues
    • Optional: citrulline on training days or daily trial for ED
    • Defer berberine/RYR until after lipid phenotype is known

    Shopping List (Concise, High Impact)¶

    Fibre + Portfolio core: Oats/barley, psyllium husk, lentils/chickpeas/beans, soy (tofu/tempeh/edamame), ground flax/chia, mixed nuts (esp. almonds/walnuts)

    Mediterranean core: Extra-virgin olive oil, tomatoes/leafy greens/crucifers, berries, citrus, garlic/onion, herbs/spices, olives

    Protein (lean + varied): Oily fish (salmon/sardines/mackerel), white fish, eggs, Greek yoghurt (if tolerated), poultry, occasional lean red meat (smaller portions), whey/casein only if needed

    Carbs for running quality: Rice, potatoes, quinoa, wholegrain bread, bananas/dates, honey (intra-run if needed)

    Joint-support foods: Extra-virgin olive oil, fatty fish, colourful veg/fruit (polyphenols), turmeric/ginger as food additions

    What to limit: Bacon/sausages/processed meats; ultra-processed snack foods; high-sat-fat "hidden" sources
