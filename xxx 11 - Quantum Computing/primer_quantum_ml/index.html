    A Primer on Quantum Machine Learning¶

    Paper: arXiv:2511.15969v1, Nov 2025 Authors: Su Yeon Chang, M. Cerezo (Los Alamos National Laboratory / Quantum Science Center)

    Opening Briefing¶

    Quantum machine learning is not one field with one problem; it is two fields that happen to share a name, and the failure to distinguish them has distorted a decade of expectations. The first strand — linear-algebraic QML built atop HHL, qPCA, and QSVE — promised exponential speedups for classical data problems like recommendation and clustering. The second strand — variational QML using parameterised quantum circuits on NISQ hardware — imported the methodology of deep learning wholesale, substituting rotation gates for weight matrices. Both strands have now run into walls that are not engineering limitations but mathematical obstructions, and understanding exactly where each wall stands is the prerequisite for any honest assessment of the field.

    The linear-algebraic programme collapsed in a specific and instructive way. Ewin Tang's 2019 dequantisation of the Kerenidis-Prakash quantum recommendation system demonstrated that the exponential speedup was an artefact of the QRAM data-access assumption: grant a classical algorithm the same L2-norm sampling access that QRAM implicitly provides, and the quantum advantage shrinks to at most polynomial. This dequantisation template has since propagated across quantum PCA, supervised classification, and clustering, deflating exponential claims to polynomial residuals in each case. Meanwhile, the variational programme faces its own fundamental barrier: barren plateaus, proven by Cerezo et al. (2021) and extended by Larocca et al. (2025), show that gradients of cost functions vanish exponentially with qubit count for deep random circuits, global observables, high entanglement, and even moderate noise. The parameter-shift rule compounds the problem — without backpropagation, each gradient component demands two full circuit evaluations, making the training cost per step linear in parameter count rather than amortised.

    The implication of these two failures taken together is that quantum advantage for classical data, if it exists at all, must be found in a narrow corridor: structured ansatze with provably trainable landscapes (quantum convolutional networks at O(log n) depth, equivariant circuits exploiting known symmetries) operating on problems whose structure quantum hardware can exploit without relying on QRAM. The one domain where QML delivers unambiguous, already-deployed advantage is quantum data — learning properties of quantum states, processes, and channels. Classical shadows, requiring only O(log M) random measurements to predict M observables, represent precisely the kind of result the field needs: provable, practical, and impossible to dequantise because the data is natively quantum.

    This document maps both strands of QML from their classical ML foundations through data embedding, variational architectures, computational learning theory, fault-tolerant primitives, and generative and reinforcement learning extensions, arriving at a frank accounting of where proven advantage exists, where it has been disproven, and where the open problems lie.

    I. Classical ML Foundations¶

    PAC Learning Framework¶

    • Probably Approximately Correct (PAC) learning (Valiant, 1984): a concept class C is efficiently PAC-learnable if a polynomial-time algorithm can, with probability >= 1 - delta, output hypothesis h with error <= epsilon, using poly(n, 1/epsilon, 1/delta) samples
    • Sample complexity and computational complexity are distinct concerns
    • Classical ML hierarchy: linear models < kernel methods < neural networks (universal approximators per Cybenko 1989, Hornik 1991)

    Why Consider Quantum?¶

    Classical Limitation Quantum Potential
    Exponential state spaces Quantum superposition encodes exponentially many states in n qubits
    Hard optimisation landscapes Quantum interference and entanglement may navigate landscapes differently
    Data access bottlenecks Quantum data structures (QRAM) enable polylogarithmic access

    II. Data Embedding and Feature Maps¶

    Key: The choice of data encoding into quantum states fundamentally determines what a quantum model can learn. It is analogous to choosing a kernel in classical ML.

    Encoding Strategies¶

    Strategy Description Complexity
    Basis encoding Map classical bitstring to computational basis state Simple but limited
    Amplitude encoding Encode N features as amplitudes of log(N) qubits Exponential compression but state-prep cost
    Angle encoding Encode features as rotation angles on individual qubits Common in variational QML
    Kernel-based Map data into quantum Hilbert space via feature map circuit U(x) Links to quantum kernel methods

    Quantum Kernels¶

    • Quantum kernel: k(x, x') = |<0| U^dag(x') U(x) |0>|^2
    • Computed by preparing states and measuring overlap (swap test or direct measurement)
    • Equivalent to running an SVM in the quantum feature space
    • Inductive bias depends entirely on the encoding circuit structure
    • Risk of exponential concentration: kernel values concentrate around a fixed value as qubit count grows, making the kernel matrix effectively trivial

    III. Variational QML (Near-Term / NISQ)¶

    Architecture: Parameterised Quantum Circuits (PQCs) / Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs)¶

    |0> --[Encoding U(x)]--[Trainable V(theta)]--[Measurement]--> classical output
    • PQC/QNN: alternating layers of parameterised rotation gates and entangling gates
    • Output: expectation value of an observable,
    • Training: classical optimiser updates theta to minimise a loss function
    • Viewed as a quantum feature model or quantum kernel method (Schuld 2021)

    Expressibility and Universality¶

    • Deep enough PQCs can approximate any unitary (universal)
    • Expressiveness != trainability -- highly expressive circuits are often untrainable
    • Overparameterisation can help avoid local minima (analogous to classical overparameterised nets)

    Challenges¶

    Challenge Details
    Barren plateaus Gradients vanish exponentially with qubit count for deep random circuits, global cost functions, high entanglement, or noise. Proven by Cerezo et al. (2021)
    Local minima Loss landscape contains many local minima and saddle points
    Shot noise Each gradient estimate requires O(1/epsilon^2) measurements
    No backpropagation Parameter-shift rule requires 2 circuit evaluations per parameter per gradient component
    Noise NISQ hardware noise degrades gradients and expressiveness; noise-induced barren plateaus proven
    Classical simulability Many shallow PQC families can be efficiently simulated classically

    Key: Barren plateaus are the central obstacle for variational QML at scale. Mitigation strategies include: local cost functions, shallow circuits, structured ansatze (equivariant circuits, convolutional QNNs), smart initialisation, and classical pre-training.

    Equivariant QNNs¶

    • Encode known symmetries (translation, rotation, permutation) directly into circuit structure
    • Constrains hypothesis space, reduces parameters, provably avoids some barren plateaus
    • Quantum convolutional neural networks (QCNNs): O(log n) depth, proven absence of barren plateaus for local cost functions (Pesah et al. 2021)
    • Geometric QML: framework using representation theory to design equivariant circuits for arbitrary symmetry groups

    IV. Quantum Computational Learning Theory¶

    Quantum PAC Learning¶

    • Quantum learner has access to a quantum example oracle providing superpositions over labelled examples
    • Provable separations exist:
    • Parity functions: Classically require exponential samples; quantum learner (Bernstein-Vazirani) solves with O(n) queries
    • DNF formulas, k-juntas: Quantum speedups proven for sample complexity
    • Discrete log-based concepts: Exponential separation under cryptographic assumptions (Shor's algorithm)

    Key: Most quantum-classical separations in learning rely on oracle or cryptographic hardness assumptions. Translating these into practical real-world advantages for classical data remains the open problem.

    Learning Quantum Concepts (Quantum Data)¶

    Task Best Known Method Advantage
    Quantum state tomography Full reconstruction; exponential in n qubits No advantage -- fundamental cost
    Classical shadows (Aaronson, Huang et al.) Random measurements + classical post-processing; predict M observables with O(log M) measurements Provable, practical, widely used
    PAC learning quantum states Predict properties from copies of unknown state Proven exponential advantages with quantum memory
    Learning quantum processes/channels Shadow tomography, process tomography variants Active research area with practical near-term applications

    Key: Classical shadows are a breakthrough: O(log M) measurements suffice to predict M linear functions of an unknown quantum state. This is already practically deployed in labs for characterising quantum devices.

    V. QML from Linear-Algebraic Primitives (Fault-Tolerant Era)¶

    Core Subroutines¶

    Primitive What It Does Runtime
    QPE (Quantum Phase Estimation) Estimates eigenvalues of a unitary O(1/epsilon) applications of U
    HHL (Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd, 2009) Solves Ax=b, producing x> proportional to A^-1 b
    qPCA (Quantum Principal Component Analysis) Extracts principal components of a density matrix Exponential speedup for low-rank matrices
    QSVE (Quantum Singular Value Estimation) Estimates singular values of a matrix Core building block for many algorithms
    QSVT (Quantum Singular Value Transformation) Applies polynomial transformations to singular values Unifying framework; subsumes HHL, QPE, Grover as special cases

    Key Assumptions and Caveats¶

    • QRAM assumption: Most speedups require efficient quantum data access via Quantum Random Access Memory, which provides superposition queries over classical data in O(polylog N)
    • State preparation: Loading classical data into quantum states is often the bottleneck
    • Output extraction: Quantum states encode answers in amplitudes; extracting full classical information can negate speedups

    Application: Quantum Recommendation System¶

    • Classical recommendation: O(poly(mnk) + poly(nk)) for m users, n products, rank k
    • Quantum recommendation (Kerenidis & Prakash, 2016): O(poly(k) polylog(mn)) under QRAM assumption
    • Uses qPCA/QSVE to perform spectral filtering and project user preference vectors onto dominant singular vectors
    • Output: sample from quantum state to identify most likely preferred product

    Dequantisation -- the Critical Caveat¶

    Key: Ewin Tang (2019) proved that the quantum recommendation system speedup is at most polynomial, not exponential, by constructing a classical algorithm with similar runtime using L2-norm sampling. This dequantisation program has challenged exponential advantage claims across many linear-algebraic QML algorithms.

    • Dequantisation replaces the QRAM assumption with an L2-norm sampling/query access assumption on classical data
    • Despite dequantisation, polynomial quantum improvements may still remain
    • New proposals seek advantages without QRAM entirely

    VI. Other Forms of QML¶

    A. Quantum Generative Models¶

    Model Architecture Key Property
    Quantum Boltzmann Machine (QBM) Transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian; thermal state sampling Extends classical BM to quantum correlations; training is BQP-hard exactly
    Quantum Circuit Born Machine (QCBM) Shallow parameterised circuit; Born-rule sampling Hardware-efficient; leverages quantum supremacy sampling hardness
    Quantum VAE (qVAE) Quantum encoder-decoder with PQC Extends classical VAE to quantum latent spaces
    Quantum GAN (qGAN) Generator and/or discriminator are quantum circuits Adversarial training in quantum setting
    • PAC learnability of distributions: Under DDH assumption, there exist distribution classes that are efficiently quantum-PAC-learnable but not classically PAC-learnable (via Shor's algorithm breaking discrete log)
    • Theoretical gap: Most quantum generative models remain heuristic with limited formal guarantees compared to supervised QML

    B. Quantum Unsupervised Learning¶

    Method Quantum Advantage Claim
    Quantum Autoencoder (QAE) Compress n-qubit states to m < n qubits; useful for state preparation
    Quantum k-means (q-means) O(k^2.5 d polylog(N)) vs classical O(kdN); reduces dependence on N
    Quantum Spectral Clustering Eigendecomposition of Laplacian: O(N) vs classical O(N^3) via QSVE/QSVT
    • Both q-means and spectral clustering advantages assume QRAM
    • Field is early-stage with limited theoretical understanding of quantum-classical gaps

    C. Quantum Reinforcement Learning (QRL)¶

    • Uses PQCs to represent Q-functions or policies: Q(s,a;theta) = <0^n| U_theta^dag(s) O_a U_theta(s) |0^n>
    • Alternative: QBM-based value-function approximation using Ising Hamiltonians
    • Provable separations exist under cryptographic assumptions (discrete log) -- quantum agent can solve environments intractable for classical RL
    • Under oracle access: polynomial-to-exponential speedups in sample/query complexity; quadratic improvements in training iterations O(T) -> O(sqrt(T))
    • Practical limitation: Most guarantees assume quantum oracle access to the environment (querying in superposition), which is unrealistic for classical environments

    VII. The Long Reaching Arm of QML¶

    Quantum Sensing / Metrology¶

    • Probe system accumulates phase under environment Hamiltonian: rho -> e^{-iH(B)t} rho e^{iH(B)t}
    • Standard quantum limit: Fisher info F_B = O(1) per shot with N independent qubits -> error epsilon needs N = Theta(epsilon^-2) samples
    • Heisenberg limit: With entanglement, F_B = O(N^2) -> error epsilon needs N = Theta(epsilon^-1) samples -- quadratic improvement
    • ML tools (Bayesian regression, variational techniques, Gaussian process regression) naturally apply to calibration and characterisation tasks
    • Expected that ML+QML will be fundamental for quantum perception -- coherent capture, storage, and processing of sensor-generated quantum states

    Quantum Compiling¶

    • Map high-level unitary U to a device-native gate sequence W_C minimising cost(C) subject to a distance metric P(U, W_C)
    • Naturally framed as a learning/optimisation problem
    • RL has been used to discover quantum circuits with variable architecture
    • QML-specific tools: Supervised learning from dataset S = {(|psi_i>, U|psi_i>)} to compile time evolutions e^{-iHt}
    • Recent advance: train on simple tensor product inputs, generalise to arbitrary states via out-of-distribution guarantees derived from QML theory

    VIII. Conclusion and Research Outlook¶

    Key: QML's two strands -- linear-algebraic (fault-tolerant) and variational (NISQ) -- both face serious roadblocks for classical data. The productive path forward requires: simpler and more interpretable quantum pipelines, explicit and auditable data-access assumptions, rigorous benchmarking against strong classical baselines, and honest reporting of negative results.

    Key: For quantum data (learning states, channels, measurements), QML is already practically successful. Classical shadows, quantum state tomography, and related protocols deliver provable and practical improvements over classical post-processing. These quantum-for-quantum successes will endure regardless of classical-data outcomes.

    Summary of Advantage Landscape¶

    Setting Status of Quantum Advantage
    Classical data, linear-algebraic QML Largely dequantized; polynomial improvements may remain
    Classical data, variational QML Barren plateaus and simulability limit scalability; no proven advantage
    Classical data, learning theory (oracle) Exponential separations exist but rely on cryptographic/oracle assumptions
    Quantum data (states, processes, channels) Proven and practical advantages; widely deployed in labs
    Quantum generative models Promising but mostly heuristic; formal guarantees limited
    Quantum RL Separations exist under oracle/crypto assumptions; practical applicability limited

    Key Open Problems¶

    1. Bridge oracle separations to real-world tasks for classical data
    2. Scalable variational training that provably avoids barren plateaus beyond shallow/local settings
    3. QRAM-free approaches to linear-algebraic QML
    4. Formal performance guarantees for quantum generative models
    5. Realistic QRL architectures that do not require quantum environment access
    6. Quantum-native generative modelling leveraging Born-rule sampling for physically motivated distributions
