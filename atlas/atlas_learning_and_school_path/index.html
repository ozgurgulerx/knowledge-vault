    Atlas Learning and School Path¶

    From playful math/science exploration (age 4-6) through grammar school preparation (age 7-11). Consistent "little and often" beats cramming. Protect the joy of learning above all.

    Early Years (Age 4-6): Math, Physics, and Play¶

    Core Principle¶

    Key: Children learn best through playful exploration -- no pressure, no drills. At 4-5, conceptual understanding and love of learning matter far more than computation speed. Play develops attention span, perseverance, concentration, and problem-solving -- stronger predictors of academic success than reading age at this stage.

    Math Puzzles and Games¶

    Category Examples Skills Built
    Jigsaw Puzzles 24-48 piece puzzles (progress to 50+) Spatial reasoning, shapes, persistence
    Pattern Blocks & Tangrams Coogam pattern blocks, wooden tangrams Visual-spatial skills, symmetry, geometry
    Counting & Number Puzzles Number board puzzles, "I Have Who Has", card game "War" Number sense, magnitude comparison
    Board Games Hi Ho! Cherry-O, Snakes & Ladders, Dominoes, Uno, memory matching Counting, one-to-one correspondence, patterns
    Logic Puzzles SmartGames (Three Little Piggies, Camelot Jr., Castle Logix); ThinkFun My First Rush Hour Logic, problem-solving, spatial insight, planning
    Construction Play LEGO DUPLO, marble runs, Magna-Tiles, Magformers, wooden blocks Stability, gravity, slope, cause-and-effect, engineering

    Digital Games & Apps (Occasional Co-Play)¶

    App Ages Key Features
    Khan Academy Kids (Free) 2-8 Adaptive; counting, addition, shapes, patterns; no ads
    Moose Math (Free) 3-7 Counting, addition, shapes, sorting; city-building reward
    Thinkrolls (Avokiddo) 3-8 Physics puzzle -- levers, ramps, buoyancy, gears
    DragonBox Numbers Preschool Montessori-like; tangible number relationships
    Todo Math 3-8 Counting, sequencing, shape ID, addition
    Reading Eggs / Teach Your Monster to Read 4-7 Phonics-based, 10 min at a time

    Key: Treat app time as occasional co-play, not a substitute for hands-on play. Sit with him and do "math talk" about what's happening on screen.

    Daily Routines with Hidden Math¶

    Routine Opportunities
    Morning Count socks/buttons, make clothing patterns, count steps
    Meals Set the table (1-to-1 correspondence), count items, compare quantities, basic division ("share fairly")
    Cooking Measure ingredients (volume, capacity), count scoops, timer (time concepts), doubling/halving
    Clean-Up Sort laundry by type/color, pair socks, count toys into bins
    Outdoor Walks Shape hunt ("I spy a circle"), count steps, compare sizes, weigh rocks, spot numbers on signs
    Shopping Count fruit into bags, find numbers on price tags, identify shapes
    Bathtime Pour/compare volumes, float-vs-sink experiments (density), backwards counting
    Bedtime Gentle riddle or counting challenge; Bedtime Math app (children who did this a few times/week showed ~3 months higher math gains after a year)

    Key: Use math language constantly -- more/less, heavy/light, near/far, first/second/third, sphere/cylinder. Children who hear more math-related conversation develop stronger math skills (Stanford DREME).

    Manipulatives and Hands-On Tools¶

    • Counters / counting bears -- physically represent numbers
    • Ten-frame (2x5 grid) -- Montessori-inspired, numbers up to 10
    • Unifix cubes / LEGO pieces -- snap-together counting, addition/subtraction
    • Abacus -- 10-bead for visualizing numbers and arithmetic
    • Montessori materials: number rods (1-10), spindle boxes, golden bead units (base-10)
    • Cuisenaire rods -- paper strips of lengths 1-10 to compare quantities
    • Magnetic tiles (Magna-Tiles, Magformers) -- geometry, symmetry, balance physics
    • Science kits -- magnet exploration, balance scale with weights, gears building set

    Science Exploration (Building Physics Intuition)¶

    • Simple experiments -- sink or float, magnet play, plant a seed, rainbow light (prism), ramp and cars
    • Nature & Museums -- Science Museum, Natural History Museum; observe bugs, weather, seasons
    • Science Reading -- Usborne "Lift-the-Flap Questions and Answers", biographies of scientists
    • Creative Construction -- recyclable materials for kites, paper airplanes, bridges (air, forces, structures)
    • Water play, sandbox building, throwing balls at different sizes/distances

    Books That Inspire Math Thinking¶

    Book Concept
    The Very Hungry Caterpillar Counting, sequencing
    Feast for 10 / Anno's Counting Book Counting to 10
    Mouse Shapes Geometric shapes
    Pattern Fish Patterns
    The Greedy Triangle Shape properties
    Quack and Count Addition combinations
    MathStart series Sorting, size comparison
    Pete the Cat and His Four Groovy Buttons Subtraction
    One Fish Two Fish Counting
    Ada Twist, Scientist Scientific curiosity

    Key: Reading math storybooks improves engagement and attitudes toward math. See DREME's list of 40 recommended math picture books.

    TV Shows and Media¶

    Show Focus
    Numberblocks (BBC) Number concepts 1-10, addition, odds/evens
    Peg + Cat (PBS) Math problem-solving
    Team Umizoomi (Nick Jr) Numbers, patterns, shapes
    Blaze and the Monster Machines STEM -- magnetism, buoyancy, trajectory
    Sid the Science Kid (PBS) Basic physics, measurement
    • Math songs: Five Little Monkeys, One Two Buckle My Shoe, The Ants Go Marching, Ten Green Bottles

    Montessori-Style Strategies¶

    1. Follow the Child's Interests -- If Atlas loves cars, use cars for counting/measuring. Let him choose. If frustration, don't force it.
    2. Hands-On and Multi-Sensory -- Use fingers for counting (research-backed "embodied learning"). Jump along a number line. Trace shapes. Draw in sand.
    3. Autonomy with Guided Discovery -- Set up "invitations to play." Pose problems but let him solve them. Avoid rushing to correct.
    4. Growth Mindset -- Say "I love how you worked hard on that!" not "You're so smart." Emphasize effort and strategies over innate ability. Encourage self-correction.
    5. Math Talk and Rich Vocabulary -- Use proper terms: sphere, cylinder, edge, corner. Ask open-ended questions: "Why do you think that tower fell over?"
    6. Keep it Playful -- Silly stories, humor, pretend scenarios. No timed drills, no insisting on writing numbers.
    7. Consistency -- Quick counting game every morning, puzzle after lunch, bedtime math ritual. Daily little activities build a "math kid."

    Printable and DIY Activities¶

    • Roll-and-cover sheets, playdough mats, bingo-style games (PreKinders, Education.com)
    • Homemade puzzles -- cut index cards for shape/number matching; origami for symmetry
    • Pattern crafts -- bead bracelets (ABAB pattern); pattern recognition is highly linked to later algebraic thinking
    • Simple logic riddles -- "I am an even number between 2 and 6. Who am I?"

    Language Development (Age 4-7)¶

    Strategies¶

    • Reading Aloud Daily -- Non-negotiable; single most powerful tool for language development
    • Library Visits -- Regular trips; let Atlas choose books; storytelling/rhyme-time sessions
    • Phonics -- Systematic approach: Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons or Jolly Phonics
    • Vocabulary Games -- "Odd one out", rhyming games, storytelling games
    • Simon Says / Following Directions -- Multi-step directions sharpen listening
    • Conversational Reasoning -- Encourage "why" questions, pose riddles and analogies
    • Role Play and Pretend -- Expands language naturally; ask open-ended questions during play

    Resources¶

    Resource Notes
    Oxford Owl (Age 4-5) Free eBooks, storytelling videos, phonics games
    Reading Eggs / Teach Your Monster to Read Phonics-based online programs
    Usborne Very First Reading Parent-child reading series
    Children's audio books Builds listening without screen time

    Non-Verbal Reasoning and Spatial Skills (Age 4-7)¶

    Strategies¶

    • Puzzles -- Jigsaws (24 to 50+ pieces); feed competitive nature ("beat his own record")
    • Construction Toys -- LEGO, Duplo, Magna-Tiles, wooden blocks, Meccano (later)
    • Pattern Blocks & Tangrams -- Classic NVR tasks
    • Visual Pattern Games -- "Complete the sequence" with shapes/colors
    • Matching and Sorting -- Categorize by color, shape, size; "what's the same, what's different"
    • Memory and Observation -- "Spot the difference", memory card games
    • Outdoor Play -- Climbing, sports, dancing, obstacle courses, treasure hunts with spatial clues

    Resources (Age 4-6)¶

    • Orchard Toys -- Shopping List, Shape Snap
    • Smart Games -- Camelot Jr., Three Little Piggies, Castle Logix
    • ThinkFun -- Rush Hour Jr. (5+), Shape by Shape
    • BrainBox Cards -- Quick educational card games

    School Path (Age 7-11): Grammar School Preparation¶

    UK 11+ Exam Overview¶

    Fact Detail
    11+ exam Tests Maths, English, and Reasoning for selective grammar school entry (~age 11)
    Competition One London school: 650 applicants for 140 places
    Grammar schools in England ~160
    Formal prep typically starts Year 4 or later (age 8-9), 1-2 years before exam

    Mathematics (Age 7-11)¶

    • Age 6-7: master addition/subtraction via games like Math Bingo
    • Use visual methods (drawing, objects) for multiplication/division (~age 8)
    • Age ~8: start word problems applying math in real contexts
    • Age 9-10: introduce practice papers/workbooks (Bond, CGP) in moderation -- "little and often"
    • Continue puzzles: magic squares, Sudoku, logic riddles

    Resources: White Rose Maths (UK curriculum aligned), NRICH (Cambridge Uni rich problems), Bond 11+, Schofield & Sims

    Verbal Reasoning (Age 7-11)¶

    • Age 6-7: Steady diet of books at right level + continue reading advanced books aloud
    • Age 8+: Vocabulary expansion -- "word of the day", dictionaries, vocabulary notebook
    • Age 9-10: Verbal Reasoning practice -- synonyms, letter sequences, verbal analogies (Bond Start Up, age 8-9)
    • Story writing -- Creative writing/journaling to practice expressing ideas

    Resources: Junior Scrabble, Bananagrams Junior (age 6-7+), Bond / CGP 11+ Verbal Reasoning (age 9-11)

    Key: Adapt to Atlas's pace -- some 4-year-olds read early, others closer to 6. Make sure he enjoys books. Love of reading will naturally carry him toward high verbal ability.

    Non-Verbal Reasoning (Age 7-11)¶

    • Logic puzzle books -- "Mind Benders" series
    • Spatial board games -- Rush Hour, Blokus, Qwirkle (~age 7+)
    • Drawing and Geometry -- Symmetrical drawings, origami
    • Age 9-10: 11+ NVR practice questions (Bond, CGP) as brain-teaser games

    Science (Age 7-11)¶

    • Follow his interests (space, dinosaurs, electricity) with deeper projects/kits
    • Age 8-10: Snap Circuits, simple chemistry sets
    • Children's science workshops at museums/community centers
    • By age 10-11: basic science concepts (states of matter, forces, solar system)

    Resources: Mystery Science (free lessons), DK encyclopedias, LEGO WeDo robotics, Lego Science kits

    Balanced Routine and Avoiding Burnout¶

    Key: Atlas is very competitive -- nurture this carefully so he doesn't become overwhelmed.

    Principle Detail
    Daily Structure Short learning periods + creative play + outdoor time + rest. ~1 hour formal "lesson" total, in chunks
    Free Play & Downtime Ample unstructured play every day
    Physical Activity Running, sports, playground -- improves concentration
    Little and Often Consistent bite-sized learning prevents cramming and reduces pressure
    Watch for Stress If frustrated, pause and return later; avoid pushing beyond limits
    No Over-Tutoring Avoid overloading near exam years; keep prep efficient not excessive
    Sleep and Nutrition Consistent bedtime and healthy meals support cognitive development

    Inspiring and Motivating Atlas¶

    • Make learning a game/competition -- Time puzzles, "teacher vs student" quizzes
    • Praise effort and progress over results; foster growth mindset
    • Small achievable goals -- "Finish this puzzle", "read one new book this week"; chart/stickers
    • Role models -- Biographies of inventors/scientists, The Magic School Bus
    • Collaborative learning -- Playdates, homeschool networks, math circles, science groups
    • Follow his interests -- Let him choose activities/subjects; builds self-motivation

    Year-by-Year Roadmap¶

    Age Focus
    4-6 Basic literacy/numeracy, love of learning through play
    7-8 More structure, introduce 11+ question exposure in fun ways
    9-10 Gradually increase targeted practice for the exam
    Final year Technique, mock exams in moderation; balanced schedule

    Key: The ultimate measure of success is not just getting into a grammar school, but developing into a happy, confident learner. Nurture competitiveness through fun challenges while ensuring plenty of time to play and be a child.

    Sources: Stanford DREME Network, University of Chicago early math studies, Montessori educational principles, Ivy Education, Koru Kids, Education Endowment Foundation, Oxford Owl, various child development experts.
