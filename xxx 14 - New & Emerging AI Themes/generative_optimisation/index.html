    Generative Optimisation: AI Search Visibility & Recommendation Influence¶

    Generative optimisation increases the probability that AI systems -- search engines, assistants, recommendation agents -- cite, surface, or recommend a brand, product, or content. Two disciplines:

    1. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO): optimising content so AI search engines cite it in synthesised answers (marketing/content side).
    2. First-party recommendation optimisation: engineering the answer stack you control -- retrieval, ranking, system instructions, fine-tuning, personalisation -- so your assistant recommends the right products (engineering/builder side).

    Both rest on the same insight: recommendations emerge from what the model attends to (context construction, retrieval, ranking) and how the system resolves ambiguity, not from "magic words."

    Opening Briefing¶

    The defining fact about generative optimisation is that it operates on both sides of the same mechanism simultaneously — and the two sides are diverging fast. On the outside, marketers optimise content so that models they cannot inspect will cite their brands; on the inside, builders engineer retrieval pipelines, ranking functions, and system instructions so that assistants they fully control recommend the right products. Both reduce to context construction: what lands in the model's attention window determines what gets cited. Yet only 12% of AI-cited sources overlap with Google's top-10 organic results, and that overlap is collapsing — from 76% to 38% between mid-2025 and early 2026 — which means the signals that earn visibility in generative engines are structurally different from those that win in traditional search.

    The empirical evidence on what actually moves citation probability is surprisingly concentrated. Three techniques — authoritative source attribution, named expert quotations, and statistics with provenance — each lift visibility 30-40% in controlled benchmarks, while keyword stuffing decreases it by 10%. Earned media dominates: 48-95% of LLM citations originate from third-party coverage rather than brand-owned pages, and brand search volume (correlation 0.334) predicts AI citation rates more reliably than backlink profiles. On the first-party side, dense retrieval reaches 65.2% top-5 accuracy against BM25's 42.9%, and the pipeline sequence — retrieve, rerank with business constraints, generate with citations — is the only reliable path to measurable recommendation lift. Contextual bandits add a further 12.5% click improvement when exploration-exploitation trade-offs are governed by multi-objective rewards that include satisfaction and complaint rates, not just conversion.

    What these observations mean together is that generative optimisation is not a marketing channel but an information-architecture discipline. The measurement crisis makes this harder to see: no tool has access to actual AI query volume, 40-60% of cited sources rotate monthly, and Nature Communications research shows 50-90% of LLM responses are not fully supported by their own cited sources. The industry is optimising toward a moving, partially hallucinated target. Meanwhile, winner-takes-most dynamics — LLMs cite only 2-7 domains per response versus Google's ten blue links — compress the viable competitive space and raise the stakes of every retrieval and ranking decision.

    This document maps the full surface area: GEO techniques and their academic evidence base, first-party recommendation engineering from prompt design through RLHF, the adversarial and regulatory landscape, and the measurement gaps that remain unsolved. The operating claim is that durable advantage accrues not to those who game citation patterns but to those who build genuine authority signals and architect retrieval stacks with auditable, multi-objective governance — because the same context-construction mechanism that makes optimisation possible also makes manipulation detectable.

    Part I: Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)¶

    What GEO is and how it differs from SEO¶

    GEO (coined in a November 2023 Princeton/IIT Delhi/Georgia Tech paper, arXiv:2311.09735, KDD 2024) optimises facts, entities, and brand signals to earn citations in synthesised AI responses -- unlike SEO which optimises pages to rank in ten blue links. Success metrics shift from rankings/CTR to citation frequency, brand mention sentiment, and share of voice.

    Terminology: GEO is broadest (82% positive sentiment among SEO thought leaders). AEO targets direct-answer platforms. LLMO centres on ChatGPT/Claude. Google's position: "good SEO is good GEO" -- yet only 12% overlap between AI-cited sources and Google's top-10 organic results.

    Market scale: GEO tools market: $886M in 2024, projected $7.3B by 2031 (34% CAGR). 90+ companies. 98% of CMOs investing in GEO strategies in 2026.

    How AI models choose what to cite¶

    All major platforms use parametric knowledge (weights) + real-time retrieval (RAG). The RAG pipeline: query fan-out → retrieve/chunk (200-500 word segments) → rank by relevance/authority/recency → synthesise with citations.

    Citation patterns by platform:

    Platform Dominant sources Key trait
    ChatGPT Wikipedia (47.9% of top-10), Bing index (87% match) 90% of citations from URLs ranking 21+ on Google
    Perplexity Reddit (46.7% of top-10) Strongest recency bias: 76.4% of top-cited pages updated within 30 days
    Google AI Overviews Reddit (21%), YouTube (18.8%), LinkedIn (13%), Quora (14.3%) Most balanced distribution

    Top-10 overlap between AI Overviews and organic results dropped from 76% to 38% (July 2025 - February 2026). Only 11% of websites earn citations from both ChatGPT and Perplexity.

    AI crawlers: OpenAI has GPTBot/OAI-SearchBot/ChatGPT-User. Anthropic has ClaudeBot/Claude-SearchBot/Claude-User. Perplexity has PerplexityBot/Perplexity-User. Bot traffic ≈ 30% of global web traffic.

    Key academic research¶

    • Princeton/IIT Delhi (GEO-Bench): 9 methods on 10K queries. Statistics: +40% visibility. Quotations: +41%. Authoritative sources: +115.1% for rank-5 sites. Keyword stuffing -10%.
    • Harvard adversarial study (arXiv:2404.07981): Strategic Text Sequences (STS) made a never-recommended product the most frequently recommended.
    • U of Toronto (arXiv:2509.08919): AI search shows "systematic and overwhelming bias towards earned media" over brand-owned content.
    • Nature Communications (April 2025): SourceCheckup -- 50-90% of responses not fully supported by cited sources across 7 LLMs.
    • Bhan/Search Atlas: 5.17M citations across 907K domains -- LLMs "reproduce the structure of the open web."

    Practitioner techniques¶

    Content architecture for AI extraction:

    • Answer capsule: comprehensive standalone answer after primary heading → cited 67% more often
    • Each paragraph should be an independently extractable unit
    • JSON-LD schema markup (Article, FAQPage, HowTo, Product) → 36% more likely in AI summaries
    • Entity-optimised content with structured data → +50% citation probability
    • Three methods consistently boost visibility 30-40%: authoritative sources, named expert quotations, attributed statistics

    Brand entity building and earned media:

    • Earned media is the primary asset. Firebrand: 48% of LLM citations from earned media vs 23% brand-owned. MuckRack: 95%. Edelman: 90%.
    • Brand search volume (not backlinks) is strongest predictor of AI citations (0.334 correlation)
    • Cross-platform presence on G2, Capterra, Crunchbase, LinkedIn, Reddit, YouTube validates authority
    • 25% of all AI citations from listicle roundups. Being mentioned+cited in third-party content → ~40% more likely to resurface

    Technical optimisation:

    • Allow retrieval/search bots; block training crawlers optionally. Blocking OAI-SearchBot = invisible in ChatGPT search.
    • llms.txt (analogous to robots.txt) tells AI what content matters
    • Server-side rendering essential -- most AI crawlers cannot execute JavaScript

    Critical caveat: No tool has access to actual AI query volume data. All use synthetic/estimated prompts. The measurement problem remains the industry's most fundamental challenge.

    The measurement crisis¶

    No Google Analytics equivalent for AI search. Proposed metrics (AIGVR, Citation Rate, AI Brand Score) -- none widely adopted.

    • 40-60% of cited sources rotate monthly
    • Negative correlation (r ~ -0.98) between ChatGPT and Google citations for commercial queries
    • AI Overviews reduce clicks by 34.5%, though cited pages earn 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks

    Market outlook¶

    Gartner: traditional search volume -25% by 2026, -50% by 2028. 58% of users replaced classic search with AI for product discovery (Capgemini). 89% of B2B buyers use generative AI as key purchasing source (Forrester). AI traffic to retailers: +520% (2024-2025). AI referral traffic converts at 4.4x traditional organic.

    GEO becomes a standard component of integrated marketing, not a replacement for SEO. Best positioned: brands building genuine authority through original research, expert content, and earned media.

    Part II: First-Party Recommendation Optimisation¶

    When you control the assistant, you can engineer the full answer stack to recommend the right products reliably, measurably, and compliantly.

    The formal objective: choose interventions to maximise utility(answer, query) + brand inclusion, subject to policy/safety/truthfulness/disclosure/cost constraints.

    Taxonomy of methods¶

    In-context control (no weight changes): prompt/context engineering, system instructions, tool use / agent orchestration, API-level decoding controls (token biasing, structured outputs).

    Retrieval, search, and ranking: RAG with hybrid BM25 + dense retrieval + filters, query rewriting, reranking via cross-encoders or LambdaMART with business constraints, snippet selection.

    Weight-level adaptation: fine-tuning (full, LoRA, QLoRA), reward modelling, RLHF / DPO.

    User-level optimisation: personalisation (preferences, memory, profiles), contextual bandits (exploration vs exploitation).

    Comparison of methods¶

    Method Effectiveness Cost Ethical risk Scalability
    Prompt & context engineering Medium Low Medium (if undisclosed bias) Medium
    System instructions Medium-High Low Medium High
    Tool use (catalogue APIs) High Medium Medium High
    RAG + reranking High Medium Low-Medium High
    API decoding controls (token bias) Medium Low Medium Medium
    Personalisation (profiles, memory) Medium-High Medium Medium-High (privacy) High
    Contextual bandits High Medium Medium (over-optimisation) High
    Fine-tuning (LoRA/QLoRA) Medium-High Medium Medium High
    RLHF / reward modelling High High Medium High

    Key mechanisms in detail¶

    Prompt and context engineering. Define a recommendation policy with task framing, constraints, ranking rubric, refusal policy, and few-shot examples. Overly strong "sell" prompts reduce trust and increase hallucination.

    System instructions. Encode non-negotiables: disclosure rules, relevance gating, source citation requirements. Characterised as a stronger steering layer than safety filters.

    Tool use and agentic orchestration. Expose product catalogue + policy tools; orchestrate via detect intent → retrieve → rerank → generate with citations → log. ReAct-style tool-augmented prompting outperforms reasoning-only or acting-only patterns.

    RAG and reranking. Product knowledge base with hybrid search + business-constraint reranking. Dense retrieval: 65.2% vs 42.9% Top-5 accuracy over BM25. Being retrieved/ranked is the prerequisite for being cited.

    Fine-tuning and RLHF. LoRA matches full fine-tuning quality with dramatically fewer parameters. QLoRA enables tuning on a single ~48GB GPU. RLHF reports ~85% human preference wins. DPO is lighter-weight than reward-model + RL. Caution: "tune to prefer my product" only acceptable if the assistant is explicitly branded, recommendations disclosed, and alternatives compared.

    Personalisation and contextual bandits. Contextual bandits balance exploration vs exploitation; large-scale deployments report 12.5% click lift. Reward = satisfaction + conversion - complaints - refunds. Governance-first: consent, explainability, opt-out.

    Metrics for recommendation lift¶

    • Inclusion rate: % of answers mentioning product when eligible
    • Inappropriate inclusion rate: % when not eligible
    • Position/prominence: #1, top-k, or footnote
    • Groundedness: recommendation backed by verifiable evidence
    • User value: satisfaction, retention, complaint rate, refund rate

    Implementation stages¶

    1. Define scope & assumptions
    2. Recommendation policy & disclosures
    3. Baseline measurement on query set
    4. Build product knowledge base
    5. Retrieval + reranking pipeline
    6. System instructions & output constraints
    7. Offline evaluation: relevance, groundedness, inclusion
    8. Security hardening: prompt injection & tool permissions
    9. Limited online rollout: A/B + guardrails
    10. Bandit optimisation with constrained actions
    11. Monitoring & audit logs: drift, complaints, outcomes
    12. Periodic review: legal/policy updates + retrain triggers

    Part III: Ethics, Security & Compliance¶

    Black-hat tactics and adversarial threats¶

    Black-hat GEO: LLM cloaking, schema misuse, fake E-E-A-T signals, SERP poisoning through mass-generated content. The Harvard STS study proved adversarial text can manipulate LLM recommendations fundamentally.

    State-backed disinformation: Operations like Pravda flood the web until chatbots repeat fabricated content. AI false claims doubled to 35% (NewsGuard). Hallucination rates: o3 at 33%, o4-mini at 48%, GPT-5 at 8.4% (Vectara).

    Prompt injection exploits lack of instruction/data separation. Data poisoning can induce failures with ~0.001% of training data corrupted. Both are attack classes, not legitimate optimisation.

    Fairness concerns¶

    • Winner-takes-most dynamics -- LLMs cite only 2-7 domains per response vs Google's 10 links
    • 80%+ citations from .com domains; country-specific domains collectively 3.5%
    • English-language bias substantial
    • Enterprise GEO monitoring costs ($2,000+/mo) create barriers for smaller organisations

    Platform defences¶

    • AI spam detection, penalties for manipulative structured data
    • Grounding requirements (overviews backed by top results with supporting links)
    • Multi-layer prompt injection mitigation (training, instruction hierarchy, red-teaming, UX confirmations)

    Legal and regulatory framework¶

    FTC endorsement guidance: Material connections affecting credibility must be disclosed clearly. Generalises to AI assistants with commercial ties. Operation AI Comply targets deceptive AI practices.

    EU AI Act: Transparency obligations for AI-generated content, machine-readable marking, disclosure duties. Consumer protection covers aggressive marketing, manipulative personalisation, dark patterns.

    Platform policies: Scaled AI content without added value = spam. Keyword stuffing to trigger AI citations incurs penalties.

    Compliance playbook¶

    1. Separation is safest: Keep sponsored placements distinct from assistant answers, clearly labelled
    2. In-answer disclosure: Transparent disclosure + relevance gating when brand inclusion occurs with commercial ties
    3. Auditable trail: Log sources retrieved, ranking features, product selection rationale
    4. Multi-objective rewards: Include satisfaction and harm metrics, not just CTR/conversion
    5. Security baseline: Constrain user input, limit output tokens, enforce tool least-privilege
