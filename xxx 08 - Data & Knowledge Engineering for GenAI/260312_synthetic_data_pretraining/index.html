    Synthetic Data for LLM Pretraining: The FinePhrase Playbook¶

    Opening Briefing¶

    LLM pretraining data has evolved through three phases: volume scaling (Wikipedia → trillion-token web crawls), quality filtering (neural classifiers like FineWeb-Edu and DCLM), and now synthetic data — rephrasing web documents through language models to produce better training fuel. The HuggingFace FineWeb team ran 90 experiments, generated over 1 trillion tokens, and spent 12.7 GPU-years to produce FinePhrase, a 486B-token open dataset that outperforms all existing synthetic data baselines. The core finding: prompt design is the single biggest lever, not model size, not source data quality, not output length.

    Key: The recipe is simple — pick a structured prompt (FAQ, Math, Table, Tutorial), use the smallest model that handles it (~1B parameters), blend with high-quality original data, and pour saved compute into volume.

    What Is Rephrasing?¶

    Rephrasing means running existing documents through a language model to produce variants that preserve meaning but change presentation. A document can be reformatted as a tutorial with worked examples, restructured as FAQ pairs, expanded with commentary, condensed into knowledge lists, or rewritten in Wikipedia style. Each transformation targets different downstream capabilities — tutorials may help step-by-step reasoning, FAQs boost question-answering, math reformulations strengthen quantitative skills (HuggingFace FineWeb Team, FinePhrase, Feb 2026).

    The design space spans three axes:

    • Rephrasing strategy — which prompt/transformation format to use
    • Generator model — which LLM does the rephrasing (family, size, generation)
    • Source data quality — whether the input documents are high-quality or noisy

    Prior work explored these in isolation. The interactions are where the interesting findings live.

    Prompt Design: The Dominant Variable¶

    Existing Prompts vs. DCLM Baseline¶

    Eight established datasets were benchmarked under identical training conditions (1.2B parameter model, 20B tokens, 12 benchmarks). DCLM, Nemotron-HQ-Synth, and REWIRE led clearly; Cosmopedia, FineWeb-Edu, Ultra-FineWeb, SYNTH, and EssentialWeb fell notably behind. DCLM became the target to beat.

    When individual prompts from the winning synthetic datasets were isolated, only Diverse QA Pairs (from Nemotron) and Guided Rewrite (from REWIRE) matched DCLM. The hit rate from prior work's prompts was low.

    Four New Prompts That Beat DCLM¶

    Nine novel prompt formats were designed. Four consistently outperformed DCLM:

    Prompt Strength Notable
    FAQ Pedagogical restructuring into Q&A pairs Strong on ARC, SQuAD
    Math Mathematical word problems from source content Only prompt that moved GSM8K (+1.5pp); largest SQuAD gain (+11.2pp)
    Table Structured tabular format Strongest ARC boost (+7.5pp over DCLM)
    Tutorial Step-by-step instructional format Only prompt improving DROP (+1.4pp)

    The winning prompts share a trait: they restructure content into pedagogically rich formats rather than just paraphrasing. Article, Commentary, Discussion, Explanation, and Narrative — which merely rephrase — did not beat DCLM.

    The Commonsense–Knowledge Trade-off¶

    A critical pattern emerged across all experiments: rephrasing trades commonsense reasoning for factual recall. Every synthetic prompt, including the four winners, falls below DCLM on HellaSwag and PIQA (commonsense), while beating it on ARC and SQuAD (knowledge and reading comprehension). The aggregate score masks this because gains on one side roughly cancel losses on the other. This trade-off is why mixing synthetic data with original data is essential — and why DCLM is the best mix-in for commonsense recovery.

    Generator Model: Smaller Is Better¶

    Model Size¶

    Across Gemma-3 sizes (270M → 27B), the 270M model underperforms, but 1B through 27B show no significant difference for simple prompts. For complex prompts like Guided Rewrite, the threshold rises to ~4B, but beyond that, scaling buys nothing. SmolLM2 (135M, 360M, 1.7B) confirms the same pattern.

    Key: Beyond ~1B parameters for simple prompts and ~4B for complex ones, bigger models do not produce better synthetic data.

    Model Family¶

    Six families tested at ~1B scale (SmolLM2, Falcon3, Qwen3, Gemma-3, Granite3, Llama-3.2): SmolLM2 consistently and clearly outperforms all others across every prompt. The advantage is largely a QA story — SmolLM2 leads by ~+10pp on SQuAD and +1–5pp on TriviaQA over the average of other families. On commonsense benchmarks, differences between families are tiny (1–2pp). The hypothesis: SmolLM2's instruction tuning data (smoltalk) included explicit rewrite tasks, giving it a head start.

    Model Generation¶

    Comparing Qwen versions 1.5 through 3: differences are small but newer versions consistently trend better.

    Practical takeaway: Use the newest, best-rephrasing ~1B model available. SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct dominated all comparisons.

    Dataset Strategy: Mix-in Matters Most¶

    Synthetic-Only Training Falls Short¶

    Pure synthetic training underperforms both DCLM alone and mixed training (synthetic + original data). Always mix synthetic with original data. The benefit of mixing is driven primarily by commonsense recovery on HellaSwag (+0.5 to +1.3pp).

    Mix-in Dataset Is a Major Performance Driver¶

    When tutorial-rephrased FineWeb-Edu-HQ was mixed with different datasets, DCLM outperformed across the board as mix-in. DCLM's commonsense recovery is remarkably stable — HellaSwag scores land in a tight 0.086–0.092 range across all 15 runs with DCLM as mix-in, regardless of synthetic configuration. DCLM essentially clamps commonsense performance to a narrow band.

    However, with low-quality source data, FineWeb-Edu-HQ overtakes DCLM as mix-in because its educational focus compensates for the rephrased output carrying less knowledge.

    Source Quality Best Mix-in Why
    High-quality DCLM Superior commonsense recovery
    Low-quality FineWeb-Edu-HQ Educational focus compensates for weaker rephrased knowledge

    Source Data Quality Is Secondary¶

    With a strong mix-in dataset, even low-quality sources produce competitive results. This opens a much larger pool of source data to draw from. Source quality matters only when the mix-in is weak.

    Diversity Does Not Compound¶

    Mixing prompts, model families, or both showed no significant improvement over the best individual configuration at 20B tokens. Performance averages rather than compounds. Diversity benefits may emerge at larger scales.

    Surprising Findings¶

    Typos in Prompts Don't Hurt¶

    The REWIRE prompt contained several typos and grammatical errors. A cleaned-up version was tested against the original at both 1B and 12B scale. Typos had no negative effect on downstream performance. For the 1B model, the typo-laden original actually performed slightly better. Prompt polish is not worth the effort (HuggingFace FineWeb Team, FinePhrase, Feb 2026).

    Template Collapse: When "Worse" Outputs Win¶

    SmolLM2 vs. Qwen3 on math word problems — Qwen3 produced beautiful, perfectly structured outputs (100% with Problem/Solution sections, 99% step-by-step, 60% with LaTeX). SmolLM2 was messier (only 68% complete solutions, wild variance in length from 4 to 4,000 tokens). Yet models trained on SmolLM2's data outperformed those trained on Qwen3's.

    The cause: template collapse. Qwen3's most common output start appeared 115 out of 1,000 samples; SmolLM2's most common appeared only 3 times. SmolLM2's quality distribution was actually reasonable — 45% excellent, 22% good, 25% partial, 8% poor — but its diversity prevented the model from memorizing templates rather than learning content.

    Key: For pretraining data, diversity beats consistency. A model that doesn't follow instructions perfectly can produce better training data than one that does.

    No Reliable Proxy Metrics Exist¶

    Neither FineWeb-Edu-score nor DCLM-score reliably predicts downstream performance for synthetic data:

    • DCLM-score difference is the best predictor (ρ = 0.61 with aggregate performance) — still only moderate, explaining ~30% of variance
    • Edu-score of rephrased output shows essentially no correlation (ρ = −0.08) with performance — and actively misleads by penalizing format changes (tables, FAQs, math notation) that actually improve training
    • Nearly every experiment increases DCLM score through rephrasing, but the slope from DCLM score to downstream performance is flat
    • WinoGrande shows zero correlation with any predictor

    There is no shortcut. You must train models and evaluate them.

    Output Length Is Irrelevant¶

    Compression ratios ranged from 0.25x (Commentary, Table) to 1.5x (Guided Rewrite at 12B). No meaningful relationship between compression ratio and performance exists. What matters is content and structure, not length.

    FinePhrase: The Production Dataset¶

    Configuration¶

    Every choice traces to an experimental finding:

    • Model: SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct — dominated all model family comparisons
    • Prompts: FAQ, Math, Table, Tutorial — the four that beat DCLM
    • Source: FineWeb-Edu sample-350BT (339M documents)
    • Inference: tp=1 with suffix-32 speculative decoding, max_tokens=2048, 100 parallel H100 GPUs via Slurm

    Scale and Efficiency¶

    FinePhrase produces 1.35 billion samples and 486 billion completion tokens from 339M source documents (four prompts × each document).

    Dataset Generator Tokens GPU Hours Tokens/GPU-Hour
    Cosmopedia Mixtral 8x7B 25B >10K <2.5M
    SYNTH Custom fine-tuned 80B 4K 20M
    REWIRE Llama-3.3 70B 400B ~352K ~1.1M
    FinePhrase SmolLM2-1.7B 486B ~14.7K ~33.1M

    FinePhrase achieves ~33M tokens per GPU hour — 30× more efficient than REWIRE and 13× more than Cosmopedia. It generates more tokens than REWIRE while using 24× less compute.

    Infrastructure (DataTrove)¶

    The pipeline runs on DataTrove with Slurm orchestration. Key production challenges at 339M documents × 100 workers × 2 weeks:

    • Bad document handling — skip_bad_requests catches malformed documents without crashing workers
    • Fast resume — Checkpoint-aware skipping so restarts don't re-parse entire history
    • Hub upload hardening — Retry logic for commit races (412 errors), transient 503s, and LFS verification failures across 100 concurrent writers
    • Per-task cache isolation — Prevents Xet cache corruption under concurrent access
    • Multi-config support — Four prompt templates produce four independent dataset configs with separate progress tracking

    The entire production run is defined in a single ~100-line Python script calling DataTrove's generate_data CLI.

    Model Collapse: A Non-Issue in Practice¶

    The common fear that synthetic training data leads to model collapse is a misconception in real pipelines. Nobody trains exclusively on own outputs without new information. Real pipelines mix synthetic with human data, use diverse reference materials in prompts, and apply synthetic data strategically. Model collapse occurs in closed loops without new signal — real synthetic data pipelines introduce new knowledge and perspectives. FineWeb found no degradation from naturally occurring AI-generated data on the web (HuggingFace FineWeb Team, FinePhrase, Feb 2026).

    Open Questions¶

    Several research directions remain unexplored:

    • Data repetition — Can you repeat data more often if repetitions are rephrased?
    • Mixing ratio — Optimal synthetic-to-original proportions (50%? 20%? 5%?)
    • Generation parameters — Impact of temperature, top_p on rephrasing quality
    • Best-of-N filtering — Generate multiple rollouts, score, keep the best
    • Source filtering — Filter documents before or after rephrasing (e.g., math prompts on non-math documents waste compute)
    • Scaling validation — Do findings hold at 100B+ token scales?
    • Automatic prompt optimization — Tools like DSPy for rephrasing prompts
    • Diffusion language models — Parallel generation for 2–10× inference speedups (LLaDA2.1-flash, Mercury 2 at 1,000+ tokens/sec on Blackwell GPUs)

    Summary Table¶

    Question Answer
    Best prompts? FAQ, Math, Table, Tutorial — structured pedagogical formats
    Model size? ~1B sufficient for simple prompts, ~4B for complex ones
    Best model family? SmolLM2 dominates across all prompts
    Synthetic-only training? No — always mix with original data
    Best mix-in? DCLM for HQ sources, FineWeb-Edu-HQ for LQ sources
    Source quality? Secondary with strong mix-in — even LQ sources work
    Prompt typos? No effect on performance
    Proxy metrics? Unreliable — must train and evaluate
    Output length? Irrelevant — content structure matters
    Diversity? Template diversity > consistency; mixing prompts doesn't compound
