    Curated Resource Library¶

    Opening Briefing¶

    The conventional framing of AI product resources treats tooling choices and design methodology as separate disciplines — an engineering reading list here, a UX philosophy there. That separation is now the primary failure mode. The document that follows reveals, through its own structure, that the collapse of the traditional design process and the rise of agentic architectures are not parallel trends but a single phase transition: the artifact-centric workflow (mockup to handoff to build) is dissolving because the underlying systems are non-deterministic, and no amount of static prototyping can capture the behavior of a model whose outputs shift with every inference call.

    Three observations anchor this claim. First, the time-allocation data in the design practice section quantifies the shift precisely — mockup work has dropped from 60-70% of a designer's time to 30-40%, while direct implementation has emerged from zero to roughly 20%. That is not a gradual rebalancing; it is a structural role change completed in under two years. Second, the resource landscape itself tells a story about velocity: frameworks like LangGraph, CrewAI, and OpenAI's Agent SDK did not exist before 2023, yet they already populate the intermediate-to-advanced training pipeline alongside decade-old staples like CRISP-DM and Lean Startup. The half-life of a "current" AI engineering skill has compressed to approximately 8-12 months, which means any team that sequences learning before building will be obsolete before it ships. Third, the governance layer — NIST AI RMF, OWASP Top 10 for LLMs, ISO 42001, the EU AI Act — has expanded faster than most teams' ability to operationalize it, creating a compliance surface area that functions as a hidden tax on iteration speed.

    The second-order consequence is that design authority now migrates to whoever controls the feedback loop, not whoever controls the artifact. When a product's behavior is emergent rather than specified, the person reviewing live model outputs and adjusting prompts, guardrails, or tool-calling schemas in real time holds more influence over user experience than the person who produced the original wireframe. This is why the document's emphasis on "build-first discovery" and the shrinking vision horizon (from ten years down to three-month experiments) are not tactical advice but a description of a new power structure.

    This resource library and the design-practice framework that closes it should be read as a single operating manual for teams navigating that transition. The first half maps the knowledge topology — which resources address which phase of the product lifecycle, from discovery through governance — while the second half codifies the workflow changes already underway at teams shipping AI-native products. Together they define a practice where learning, building, and evaluating are concurrent rather than sequential.

    Design Practice in the AI Era¶

    Merged from: 260305_O_design_at_the_times_of_AI.md

    The Core Thesis: The Classic Design Process Is Collapsing¶

    Old model: Research → Discovery → Mockups → Prototype → Iterate → Ship. Designers owned the artifact (mockups).

    New model: Idea → Engineer builds rough version → Designers guide execution → Iterate live. Designers no longer control the process through mocks; they shape execution while the product is already being built.

    Two Types of Design Work Emerging¶

    1. Execution Support — Designers help engineers ship: review builds, guide UX decisions, polish interactions, fix last-mile details, ensure coherence across features. Design becomes real-time collaboration with engineering.

    2. Direction Setting — Designers provide short-term product direction. Vision horizon shrinks from 2-10 years to 3-6 months. Sometimes it's just a prototype pointing people in the right direction. AI capabilities change weekly; roadmaps become obsolete quickly.

    Designers Must Work Inside Code¶

    Designers now implement UI details themselves, edit CSS or front-end code, polish features directly in the codebase. Not full engineering — but implementation literacy. Workflow: Engineer builds v1 → Designer opens repo → Tweaks spacing/behavior → Ships improvement.

    Design Time Allocation Shift¶

    Activity Previous Now
    Mockups / prototyping 60-70% 30-40%
    Engineer pairing 20% 30-40%
    Implementation — ~20%
    Coordination 10% ~10%

    Design is far more operational.

    AI Product Development Strategy¶

    Because LLMs are non-deterministic, classic design fails — you cannot fully mock the experience. Instead: Build → Test with real model → Observe users → Adjust. Real product experimentation replaces design theory.

    "Build Trust Through Speed"¶

    Ship early, call it "research preview", iterate fast, respond to feedback. Early release only works if improvement is visible. Early launch + no improvement = loss of trust.

    Designers Should Teach Engineers Design Principles¶

    Instead of "move this button" → explain why it exists, what behavior users expect, what research says. Goal: engineer internalizes design logic, next feature improves automatically. This is design knowledge transfer.

    Figma Still Matters (Even in AI Era)¶

    Coding tools are linear (iterate → iterate). Figma enables parallel exploration (8-10 options quickly, compare visually, refine micro-details). Coding tools are poor at divergent exploration.

    Three Designer Archetypes for AI Teams¶

    1. Strong Generalists — Block-shaped skillset across design, product thinking, engineering understanding, UX, strategy. Each skill at ~80th percentile.
    2. Deep Specialists — T-shaped. Visual design, technical UX, interaction systems, AI interface design. These create product differentiation.
    3. "Crack New Grads" — High curiosity, fast learners, low process baggage, builder mentality. The field is changing so fast that experience can become inertia.

    Chat Interfaces May Persist Longer Than Expected¶

    Talking scales across intelligence levels. Chat offers infinite interaction flexibility. Future likely: Chat + Generated UI (e.g., Claude generating widgets dynamically).

    The "Illegible Idea" Principle¶

    Breakthrough ideas often start illegible (strange, unclear, frontier) vs. legible (obvious, understandable). Watch for prototypes that feel confusing but generate excitement — then investigate deeper. Designers act like VCs evaluating ideas.

    Practical Takeaways¶

    • Build-first discovery — prototype > document
    • Designers must understand implementation — UI, code, model behavior
    • Product direction horizon shrinks — 10 year vision → 3 month experiments
    • Trust comes from iteration speed — users trust products that improve weekly
    • Look for "illegible" ideas — if confusing but exciting, dig deeper
