    AGI¶

    Opening Briefing¶

    AGI discourse tends to fragment into a thousand separate risk categories — alignment failure, misuse, power concentration, arms races, job displacement. A more compressed framing treats these not as independent problems but as symptoms of a single meta-dynamic: pessimization, the tendency for systems to start acting against the interests of their own members. This concept, introduced by Richard Ngo, inverts the optimization framing that dominates AI safety — the deepest risk is not that AI optimizes too well for the wrong objective, but that civilizational systems (with or without AI) degrade into self-destructive equilibria through identifiable social mechanisms.

    Pessimization: The Self-Destruction Framework¶

    The Two-Axis Model¶

    Richard Ngo frames possible futures along two dimensions (Richard Ngo, PostAGI, "Self-Destruction"):

    1. Centralized vs distributed power — who holds decision-making authority
    2. Whether the coalition cares about its members — whether the system serves or harms its participants

    These axes produce four states:

    Cares about members Does not care about members
    Centralized Benevolent dictator Ruthless dictator
    Distributed Ethical society Ruthless competition / multipolar trap

    Why Multipolar Traps Are Overrated¶

    The standard AI safety concern about distributed power is the multipolar trap — competing agents racing to the bottom, each forced to adopt harmful strategies because others will. Ngo argues this is overrated as the primary threat. The deeper danger is when a coalition becomes actively self-harming even without a single dictator or competitive race forcing it. This emergent self-harm is pessimization — the opposite of optimization. Systems do not merely fail to optimize for human welfare; they develop internal dynamics that actively work against it.

    Mechanisms of Pessimization¶

    Ngo identifies four pathways through which systems degrade into self-destruction:

    Internal Conflict¶

    Factions within a coalition hurt each other until the larger system harms itself as a whole. The damage is not from external competition but from internal predation — each faction optimizes against its neighbors rather than for the collective.

    Negative Polarization¶

    Identities become defined by opposition to the other side rather than by serving real interests. Once identity is constructed around "not-them" rather than "for-us," the system's energy redirects from value creation to mutual destruction. Political systems are the obvious example, but the dynamic appears in corporations, research communities, and standards bodies.

    Loyalty Commitments¶

    The most original element of the framework. People prove loyalty by hurting themselves, and the self-harm rewires their identity. This is not merely costly signaling — it is identity-shaping sunk-cost machinery.

    Historical examples: initiation rituals, hazing, footbinding. In each case, the pain is not incidental; it is the mechanism by which the person becomes psychologically invested in the system that demanded it.

    Modern application: Ngo argues that college often functions less as skill formation or credentialing and more as a loyalty commitment mechanism. People invest enormous time and money, then internalize elite status hierarchies, political orientations, and contempt structures that lock them into the system. The sunk cost becomes identity.

    Vice Commitments¶

    An extension of loyalty commitments into ethical territory. In corporations and elite institutions, advancement can require willingness to lie, conceal, or violate moral norms. Unethical behavior becomes a bonding mechanism — shared transgression creates mutual vulnerability that functions as trust.

    Once vice commitments take hold, the institution develops active defenses against ethical behavior: whistleblowers are punished, dissent is reframed as disloyalty, and integrity becomes a career risk. The system immunizes itself against correction.

    Key: The loyalty/vice commitment pair is the framework's sharpest tool. It explains why organizations that start with good intentions can develop self-destructive cultures that resist reform — the very mechanisms that create cohesion also create corruption, and by the time the corruption is visible, the people who could fix it have been selected against.

    Prescription: Virtue Ethics as Defense¶

    Ngo's prescription is that the antidote to pessimization is virtue ethics — not abstract consequentialism or rule-based frameworks, but individual character traits that resist coalition-level corruption:

    • Courage — willingness to dissent when the group demands conformity
    • Honesty — refusal to participate in institutional deception
    • Integrity — maintaining ethical standards when advancement requires abandoning them

    Healthy organizations need people whose character prevents loyalty/vice spirals from becoming dominant. This is a structural argument, not a moral one: virtue-possessing individuals act as circuit breakers in the degradation process.

    Diagnostic Lens¶

    The framework's best reusable output is a three-question diagnostic for assessing any organization:

    1. What behaviors get promoted? — do advancement criteria reward competence or loyalty/vice?
    2. What self-harm is rebranded as loyalty? — what sacrifices are demanded that serve the system's cohesion rather than its mission?
    3. What ethical lines must be crossed to rise? — are there implicit vice commitments embedded in the advancement path?

    Assessment¶

    The concept of pessimization is genuinely useful — it compresses political, institutional, and organizational failure into a single analytical lens. The most novel contribution is loyalty commitments as identity-shaping machinery, which explains why sunk costs create not just reluctance to leave but active defense of the system that demanded them. The framework is stronger than some of its specific applications — certain examples are politically loaded and under-argued — but the structural mechanisms stand independently of any particular illustration.
